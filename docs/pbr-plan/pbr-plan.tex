\section{Goal}

Build a playable Silent Hill 1 style town level --- a fog-bound network of watertight streets
under a low virtual sky, with enterable interiors --- that loads in the customised ioquake3 as
\textbf{ordinary Quake 3 content}, and extend the authoring and rendering pipeline until it looks
the way the reference does.

The level is a Quake~3 map, not a new game. One engine binary, one gamepack, one BSP format, stock
\texttt{scripts/*.shader} materials, and the editor's physical light entities. It is a \textbf{new} level, authored
from the start with the enhancements in mind; enhancing existing Quake~3 levels is not part of this
plan (§\ref{sec:prior}).

The compiled level has three targets, in order of precedence:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Authoring.} NetRadiant-custom under \texttt{Q3.game} creates and edits it, including
  its PBR materials as text, and q3map2 compiles it, with no other gamepack involved.
\item \textbf{Stock.} An unmodified ioquake3, with \texttt{opengl1} or its default
  \texttt{opengl2}, at default cvars runs the same pk3 as plain lightmapped Quake~3 content:
  a lightmap baked by \texttt{sh-baker} inside q3map2 (Stage~4), base textures, vanilla shader
  stages, fog volumes. It uses \textbf{no rend2
  features} --- no normal or specular maps, no \texttt{r\_pbr}, no HDR lighting files (B6 was dropped; see §\ref{sec:rend2facts}). This is what makes
  the level uploadable to \texttt{lvlworld.com}, and it should look as faithful to the enhanced
  target as a lightmap allows.
\item \textbf{Enhanced.} The customised ioquake3 with \texttt{renderer\_sh} (the new sh-renderer)
  runs the same pk3 with direct PBR, shadow maps and SH indirect. PBR levels are developed for
  this target.
\end{enumerate}
Original, non-PBR Quake~3 levels are unaffected: they keep running on \texttt{opengl2}. Every
enhancement is ignored by the stock target. None replaces stock data. §\ref{sec:contract} turns this
into rules every stage must satisfy.

\section{What changed in revision 8}\label{sec:rev8}

Decided 2026-11-21. \texttt{renderer\_sh} is built for direct invocation by ioquake3 only, and the
sky becomes a light source. Stage~3 is restructured around that.

\begin{description}[style=nextline]
\item[\texttt{renderer\_sh} is sh-renderer grown into an ioquake3 module.] The existing
  \texttt{ioq3-custom/sh-renderer} becomes a renderer module loaded by \texttt{cl\_renderer sh}
  through \texttt{GetRefAPI}, beside \texttt{opengl1} and \texttt{opengl2}, which stay untouched.
  The engine glue goes in \texttt{ioq3/code/renderersh/} and \texttt{cmake/renderer\_sh.cmake}
  (neither exists yet). sh-renderer changes go as pull requests to
  \texttt{github.com/DaviesX/sh-renderer}, which the user reviews and merges; nothing is pushed or
  opened without asking, as with \texttt{sh-baker}.
\item[Direct invocation only; the glTF path is deleted.] There is no compatibility with the
  exporter $\to$ glTF $\to$ sh-renderer path. C0 deletes from sh-renderer: the glTF loader
  (\texttt{loader.cpp}'s glTF parts and \texttt{loader\_layers\_test}), tinygltf, tinyexr, the
  EXR/xatlas SH atlas input, \texttt{SH\_material\_layers} parsing, \texttt{kLightIntensityScale}
  (\texttt{loader.cpp:18}), \texttt{ComputeLightRadius} (\texttt{scene.cpp:585--590}), the stray
  Embree include (\texttt{scene.h:3}), the unused glTF \texttt{AreaLight}
  (\texttt{scene.h:169--177}, \texttt{loader.cpp:757--793}) and the analytic sky for maps. The
  glTF loader is not kept for sh-renderer's own tests; they switch to synthetic inputs. Revision
  7's ``its glTF loader stays for its own tests and tools'' is withdrawn.
\item[The Q3 layer compositor is reference code.] The compositor already on sh-renderer's main
  (PRs \#15--\#18: \texttt{q3\_layer.h}, \texttt{q3\_layer\_bind}, \texttt{q3\_composite.glsl},
  per-layer descriptor SSBOs) is reused. Under \texttt{renderer\_sh} it is fed from shader text,
  and suspected bugs are fixed there. sh-renderer PRs \#19 (cutout coverage) and \#20 (additive
  pass) were closed as superseded; their features are redone from shader text in C2, C3 and C5.
\item[Parser decided: \texttt{renderer\_sh} writes its own.] A small BSP and shader loader in raw
  Z-up map units over \texttt{ri.FS\_*}. It does not link \texttt{libioq3\_map}, which reads only 5
  of the 17 lumps, knows no \texttt{qer\_pbr\_*}, \texttt{alphaFunc} or \texttt{rgbGen const}, and
  pulls in glog, gflags, Embree and tinygltf. \texttt{renderergl2}'s \texttt{tr\_bsp},
  \texttt{tr\_shader} and \texttt{tr\_curve} are the reference for meaning. The open decision of
  revision 4 is closed and no longer blocks C2.
\item[GL context: requested, not taken over.] ioquake3 has no existing context to take over: each
  renderer module calls \texttt{GLimp\_Init} (\texttt{sdl\_glimp.c} is compiled into every module,
  \texttt{cmake/renderer\_common.cmake:15--18}; \texttt{renderergl2/tr\_init.cpp:264--267}), and
  \texttt{sdl\_glimp.c} asks for at most GL~3.2 core (\texttt{:545--548}). sh-renderer needs 4.6
  core with a debug context and an sRGB framebuffer (\texttt{window.cpp:72--78}, \texttt{:114}).
  \texttt{sdl\_glimp.c} gains a context-request parameter that only \texttt{renderer\_sh} sets;
  \texttt{opengl1} and \texttt{opengl2} request exactly what they do today. This replaces the
  \texttt{r\_glMajorVersion}-style cvars designed in \texttt{ioq3-custom/docs/c\_interface\_design.tex}
  (\texttt{:298--300}), and the earlier wording ``initialise against an already-current GL
  context''.
\item[Sky environment light.] Missing in all three tracks until now: the point bake reads no
  environment, the editor preview has only a constant ambient, and sh-renderer's sky term is a flat
  colour times baked visibility. The map's sky shader now emits, with the same derivation as
  emitters, in the units of §\ref{sec:units}. The indirect bake records only sky visibility, never
  sky radiance, so \texttt{renderer\_sh} can change the sky without a rebake: it projects the sky to
  SH at load and multiplies its irradiance by the baked visibility (C6). The full bake adds the
  sky's radiance, so the stock lightmap gets direct and bounced sky light (B11 \texttt{sh-baker} PR,
  B12 q3map2). Side file v3 adds visibility per grid probe (B13). The editor preview's sky light is
  unshadowed (A12). Sky still never casts. §\ref{sec:sky}.
\item[Track C restructured.] C0 cleans sh-renderer for the module; C1 brings the module up; C2 is
  Tier~1; C3 splits into C3a (sky and fog) and C3b (the rest of Tier~2); C4b loads the side file,
  ahead of C5 and C6; C8 is the spot-flashlight ABI, gated by the game-code decision. 3.4 follows
  Tier~1. Light units are no longer open: the editor's physical model is authoritative. Corrected
  in passing: \texttt{refexport\_t} has 30 entry points, not 37 (\texttt{tr\_public.h:37--117};
  \texttt{A3D\_RenderGeometry} is compiled out).
\end{description}

\section{What changed in revision 7}\label{sec:rev7}

Kept for the record. Revision 8 keeps its light decisions; \texttt{renderer\_sh} embeds its own
copy of the LTC tables (4.5).

Emissive surfaces join the one light model. Revision 6 planned runtime light from emissive quads
only in \texttt{renderer\_sh} (4.5), so the editor could neither author nor preview it, and 3.4's
parity rule could not cover it. Revision 7 makes area lights editor lights, like the other three.

\begin{description}[style=nextline]
\item[Area lights are editor lights (2.2 step 6).] A new point entity, \texttt{light\_area}, is a
  one-sided rectangle: \texttt{origin} at its centre, \texttt{angles} giving the emitting normal,
  \texttt{width} and \texttt{height} in map units, and \texttt{intensity} as radiance. The editor
  previews it with LTC (linearly transformed cosines), and \texttt{renderer\_sh} evaluates the same
  model (4.5). §\ref{sec:units}.
\item[Attached by geometry, not by reference.] ``Create area light from selected face'' copies the
  face's rectangle into the entity; the face itself stays in worldspawn and is drawn and lightmapped
  as before. A brush entity owning the face was rejected: q3map2 compiles it into an inline model,
  which stock ioquake3 frees with the entity, so the glowing quad would vanish on vanilla. A face
  reference was rejected: Quake~3 faces have no names, and \texttt{target} links only entities.
\item[Intensity is derived from the shader.] The command sets the area light's radiance to the
  face's mean emissive radiance, $\bar L = \texttt{qer\_pbr\_emissiveStrength} \times
  \texttt{rgbGen const} \times \text{mean linear texel over the face's UV footprint}$, split into
  \texttt{intensity} (the largest component) and \texttt{\_color}. A proxy's flux is
  $k\pi\sum_f \bar L_f A_f$ over the selected faces, with $k=1$ for opaque emitters and $k=2$ for
  all-additive ones (two-sided billboards). ``Re-derive'' repeats it after the shader changes.
\item[Proxies for everything else.] Non-planar or non-rectangular emitters get an ordinary
  \texttt{light} or \texttt{light\_spot} with \texttt{\_proxy 1}, created from the selection with
  the derived flux. Runtime and preview treat a proxy as the light it is.
\item[The bake reads the surface, not the attachment.] \texttt{sh-baker} already turns every
  emissive surface into an area light, so \texttt{-shbake} ignores \texttt{light\_area} and
  \texttt{\_proxy} lights; otherwise the full bake would count the emitter twice. The full bake keeps
  the emitter's direct light, because the stock lightmap has no other source for it; the indirect
  bake drops it (\texttt{sh-baker} \#66), so \texttt{renderer\_sh} counts it once, from the attached
  light. No per-emitter bake switch exists or is needed; revision 6's ``if the author enables baking
  for the emitter'' is withdrawn. q3map2's placeholder \texttt{-light} skips both too.
\item[Unattached emitters warn.] An emitter with no attached light still glows on every target and
  bounces light on the enhanced one, but lights its surroundings directly only in the stock
  lightmap. \texttt{-shbake} warns once per shader with the count of such surfaces. It is a warning,
  not a stock-check failure: small glows need no light.
\item[The preview draws every emitter in linear HDR.] Emitters the preview does not light --- all
  stages additive, or \texttt{surfaceparm nolightmap} --- are drawn with the same sRGB decode and
  \texttt{qer\_pbr\_emissiveStrength} as lit ones, instead of the textured-mode image.
\item[Sidedness.] Area lights are one-sided. \texttt{sh-baker}'s area lights are two-sided
  (\texttt{light.h:220}, kept for billboard flames), so a free-standing opaque emissive quad also
  lights its back in the bake. Documented difference; 0.3 judges it.
\item[Shadows by frustum.] \texttt{\_frustum} (half-angle in degrees, default 0 = none) gives the
  area light a perspective shadow map from the rectangle's centre along its normal, with the caster
  rule, in the preview and at runtime. Without it the area light does not cast.
\end{description}

\section{What changed in revision 6}\label{sec:rev6}

The compiler does not have to reproduce any original look: there are no original levels to
rebuild, and what ships is the pk3. It must run on vanilla \texttt{opengl1}/\texttt{opengl2} and
carry everything \texttt{renderer\_sh} needs. Within that, the easiest path in the editor wins.

\begin{description}[style=nextline]
\item[Lights: the editor's physical model, not q3map2's.] The light entities the editor already
  previews --- \texttt{light} (point; \texttt{intensity} radiant flux, \texttt{radius} cutoff),
  \texttt{light\_spot} (\texttt{intensity}, \texttt{radius}, \texttt{cone}, \texttt{cone\_inner},
  \texttt{target} or \texttt{angles}) and \texttt{light\_sun} (irradiance \texttt{intensity},
  \texttt{angles}), with \texttt{\_shadows} on spots and suns --- are the one light model, shared by
  the editor preview, \texttt{sh-baker} and \texttt{renderer\_sh}. Point lights never cast; one sun
  is honoured. Keys keep their names. There is no q3map2 light model to copy, no constant $K$ and
  no calibration against q3map2 lightmaps. The editor does not read \texttt{q3map\_sun}.
  §\ref{sec:units}.
\item[\texttt{Q3.game} gets the PBR entity module (A6).] A tracked overlay in
  \texttt{setup/data/gamepacks/pbr/} installs \texttt{games/Q3.game} --- the downloaded one with
  \texttt{entities="pbr"} --- and \texttt{Q3.game/baseq3/\_pbr\_lights.ent}, defining
  \texttt{light}, \texttt{light\_spot} and \texttt{light\_sun} with the physical keys. Its name
  sorts before \texttt{entities.ent}, and the first entity class of a name is kept, so its
  \texttt{light} wins. It is installed with \texttt{sh install-gamepack.sh setup/data/gamepacks/pbr
  install/gamepacks} after the downloaded packs. A6 is folded into
  \texttt{q3-shader-pbr-materials}.
\item[Vanilla compatibility of the light entities.] The stock game prints \texttt{light\_spot
  doesn't have a spawn function} (and the same for \texttt{light\_sun}) once per entity
  (\texttt{code/game/g\_spawn.c:281}) and discards it; \texttt{light} is freed silently by
  \texttt{SP\_light}. Harmless; the stock check allowlists the line. \texttt{-keeplights} keeps every
  light in the entity lump for \texttt{renderer\_sh}. q3map2 treats any classname prefixed
  \texttt{light} as a light (\texttt{light.cpp:330}), so its placeholder lightmap uses its own
  reading of them.
\item[\texttt{sh-baker} writes the vanilla lightmap.] Stage~4.3 always writes irradiance into the
  BSP lightmap pages, plus the light grid and vertex colours, from the same lights, besides the SH
  side file. This is no longer optional or conditional on 0.3. (4.3 now does this with a separate
  full bake, on by default; the side file takes an indirect-only bake.) The irradiance-to-byte encoding is
  ours to choose so the result looks good on vanilla, for example a roll-off rather than a hard clip
  at the overbright limit. Until 4.3 lands, q3map2 \texttt{-light} produces a placeholder lightmap
  that is not expected to match the preview. 0.3 measures the \texttt{sh-baker} lightmap after 4.3.
\item[q3map2 stays the host.] BSP, \texttt{-meta}, \texttt{-vis}, lightmap UV pages, light-grid
  points and vertex lists remain q3map2's; only its lighting maths (\texttt{-light}) is replaced.
\item[Emissive strength has no Quake~3 scale.] No scale constant $S$ and no
  \texttt{include/pbrunits.h}: emissive radiance is texel $\times$ \texttt{rgbGen const} colour
  $\times$ \texttt{qer\_pbr\_emissiveStrength}, added to the base pass in the light passes' linear
  units.
\item[The editor's shadow-caster rule is authoritative.] Never cast: sky, fog, hint, trigger,
  areaportal, \texttt{noshadows}, player and bot clip, water, slime, lava, and \texttt{trans} without
  \texttt{alphashadow}. Solid
  caulk casts; alpha-tested shaders cast through their preview alpha test; other drawn opaque
  surfaces cast. \texttt{sh-baker} follows the editor; nothing claims to mirror q3map2.
\item[\texttt{pbr.game} stays frozen; its light entities move.] A7 still deletes \texttt{pbr.game}
  and the \texttt{.mtr} parser but keeps the \texttt{pbr} entity module, which \texttt{Q3.game}
  now uses.
\item[Work tracking.] \texttt{q3-stock-lights} is deleted. The changes are
  \texttt{q3-shader-pbr-materials} (A1--A3, A6) and \texttt{q3-bench-and-stock-check} (A4, A5). The
  benchmark keeps its lights as they are; only \texttt{bench.mtr} becomes
  \texttt{scripts/bench.shader}, with no \texttt{q3map\_sun} conversion and no brightness re-tune in
  the stock engine (brightness is tuned in the editor preview). The stock check drops calibration
  mode. It still compiles with \texttt{-meta -keeplights}, \texttt{-vis -saveprt}, \texttt{-light
  -patchshadows}; the last gives the placeholder lightmap.
\item[Engine parity.] 3.4 is parity with the editor's physical light model. The \texttt{-shbake}
  stage learns \texttt{qer\_pbr\_*} and the editor's caster rule.
\end{description}

\section{What changed in revision 5}\label{sec:rev5}

Kept for the record. Revision 6 supersedes its light decisions --- q3map2's model, the constant
$K$ and its calibration, \texttt{q3map\_sun} suns, \texttt{\_sh\_shadows} and the emissive scale
$S$ --- along with the stock-engine brightness tuning of the benchmark, the optional lightmap write
and the \texttt{q3-stock-lights} change (§\ref{sec:rev6}).

\begin{description}[style=nextline]
\item[PBR levels target sh-renderer only; rend2's PBR features are not used.]
  rend2 is built around the lightmap and cannot run a BRDF over metallic--roughness, occlusion and
  normal maps the way sh-renderer does. Revisions 3 and 4 shipped data for it anyway (a
  \texttt{normalMap} stage, a generated \texttt{\_s} pack, \texttt{lm\_*.hdr} calibrated to rend2).
  That is withdrawn. PBR data lives only in top-level \texttt{qer\_pbr\_*} keywords, including the
  normal map (\texttt{qer\_pbr\_normal}), and shader stages stay vanilla. Original non-PBR levels
  fall back to \texttt{opengl2} as before.
\item[The stock target is the lvlworld fallback, and \texttt{opengl1} is back in.]
  With no rend2 keywords in shader text, \texttt{renderergl1} no longer rejects the shaders, so the
  stock check runs both \texttt{opengl1} and \texttt{opengl2} at default cvars. Its fidelity comes
  from q3map2's lightmaps; §0.3 measures how close it gets.
\item[One pk3.] The base/overlay split and the rend2-PBR pack are dropped. One pk3 carries the BSP,
  shaders, base textures, lightmaps, PBR maps and, later, the SH side file. Stock renderers never
  reference the PBR files.
\item[The normal-map syntax of revision 4 was wrong anyway.] rend2 has no \texttt{normalMap
  <image>} keyword: a normal map is a stage containing \texttt{stage normalMap} and \texttt{map
  <image>}, and the bare form makes rend2 reject the shader (\texttt{renderergl2/tr\_shader.cpp:870},
  \texttt{:1267}).
\item[Lights: q3map2's model, exactly, scaled by one constant.] The editor preview reproduces
  q3map2's falloff, spot cone and suns and multiplies by one constant $K$ derived from the lightmap
  path; \texttt{renderer\_sh} adopts the same model (3.4). q3map2's \texttt{light}$\times 7500$ is a
  radiant \emph{intensity}, not a flux. Point lights do not cast shadows in the preview. The
  per-light shadow switch is \texttt{\_sh\_shadows}. §\ref{sec:units}.
\item[Compile flags.] \texttt{-keeplights} is a BSP-stage option (\texttt{bsp.cpp:658}), not a light
  option; \texttt{-patchshadows} is added to \texttt{-light} because q3map2's Quake~3 default leaves
  patches unshadowed while the preview's patches cast.
\item[Work tracking.] Tasks A1--A6 are specified as three openspec changes:
  \texttt{q3-shader-pbr-materials} (A1--A3), \texttt{q3-bench-and-stock-check} (A4--A5) and
  \texttt{q3-stock-lights} (A6).
\end{description}

\section{What changed in revision 4}\label{sec:rev4}

Kept for the record. Revision 5 supersedes its use of rend2's \texttt{normalMap} stage, the
overlay pk3 and \texttt{lm\_*.hdr} (§\ref{sec:rev5}); revision 6 supersedes its handing of q3map2's
parsed lights to the baker (§\ref{sec:rev6}).

\begin{description}[style=nextline]
\item[The export pipeline was a prior attempt, not the pipeline.]
  \texttt{ioq3-map-exporter} and \texttt{ioq3-materialize} were built to export existing Quake~3
  levels and enhance them with no editor in the loop. That did not work well: those levels are
  inherently incompatible with the modern pipeline. Revisions 2 and 3 treated both tools as live
  consumers and drew conclusions from them --- that \texttt{.mtr} ``has no consumer'', that PBR
  maps would be generated and found by file-name suffix, that the exporter's light scale was part
  of the chain. Those conclusions are withdrawn. The move onto Quake~3 shader conventions stands,
  for a different and stronger reason: the stock target cannot load anything else.
\item[PBR materials are authored as text in \texttt{.shader} files.]
  As original NetRadiant does for Quake~3 shaders, with no material GUI. Roughness, metallic,
  occlusion and their factors are top-level \texttt{qer\_pbr\_*} keywords; the normal map is rend2's
  own \texttt{normalMap} stage; base colour and emissive are ordinary stages. §\ref{sec:materials}.
\item[The prefix is \texttt{qer\_pbr\_}.]
  ``qer'' is QuakeEd Radiant, the namespace for editor hints. Both ioquake3 renderers skip any
  top-level keyword starting with \texttt{qer} (\texttt{renderergl2/tr\_shader.cpp:1718}), and
  q3map2 ignores unknown \texttt{qer\_} keywords while it warns on unknown \texttt{q3map\_} ones
  (\texttt{shaders.cpp}, ``Unknown q3map\_* directive''). The keywords are read by the editor,
  q3map2's SH stage and \texttt{renderer\_sh}; the prefix is chosen because stock engines skip it.
\item[The suffix convention of revision 3 is withdrawn.]
  Maps are named explicitly in shader text. What remains is a naming hazard: stock rend2
  auto-loads \texttt{<image>\_n}, \texttt{\_nh} and \texttt{\_s} siblings even without a keyword,
  so those suffixes are reserved (§\ref{sec:materials}).
\item[SH baking moves into q3map2.]
  The prior pipeline baked SH into a single xatlas atlas over an exported glTF and rendered that
  glTF; nothing ever mapped back to the BSP. Revision 4 links \texttt{sh-baker} into q3map2 as a
  library and bakes into q3map2's own lightmap pages, through a new \texttt{-shbake} stage.
  \texttt{sh-baker} gains a library API that serves both xatlas atlases and multi-page layouts,
  delivered as reviewed pull requests to its repository. Stage~4 is rewritten.
\item[The exporter's light conversion leaves the chain.]
  q3map2 hands its own parsed lights to the baker, so the q3map2 interpretation reaches the bake
  directly. The units section drops the exporter row.
\item[Open: parser reuse for \texttt{renderer\_sh}.]
  Revision 2 planned for \texttt{renderer\_sh} to consume the exporter's parsing code
  (\texttt{libioq3\_map}). With the exporter out of the pipeline this is a decision, not a given.
  Decided by revision 8: \texttt{renderer\_sh} writes its own (§\ref{sec:rev8}).
\end{description}

\section{Earlier revisions}

\textbf{Revision 3} made the three targets explicit; dropped \texttt{renderergl1}, which rejects a
whole shader on an unknown keyword (\texttt{renderergl1/tr\_shader.cpp:972}, \texttt{:1588},
default shader at \texttt{:2491}); froze \texttt{pbr.game} and made \texttt{Q3.game} the only
authoring target; found that a PBR specular map is not stock-safe; made q3map2's light
interpretation authoritative (withdrawn by revision 6); required a complete BSP lightmap; recognised that stock rend2 loads
\texttt{lm\_*.hdr}; added the base/overlay packaging and the stock-engine check. Its
material-suffix convention was withdrawn by revision 4; dropping \texttt{renderergl1}, shipping
\texttt{lm\_*.hdr} and the base/overlay packaging are withdrawn by revision 5
(§\ref{sec:rev5}). The rest stands.

\textbf{Revision 2} reframed the target as a Quake~3 level rather than a new game; moved lights onto
the stock \texttt{light}/\texttt{target}/\texttt{q3map\_sun} convention (withdrawn by revision 6);
found that
\texttt{-keeplights} already exists (\texttt{bsp.cpp:658}, persisted as \texttt{\_keepLights} at
\texttt{writebsp.cpp:230}); measured what rend2 already ships; decided that \texttt{sh-renderer}
becomes \texttt{renderer\_sh} (§3.1); and re-ordered the work so that fog, darkness and the
flashlight come before SH indirect. Its rationale for retiring \texttt{.mtr} rested on the export
pipeline and is replaced by revision 4's.

\textbf{Retained from the completed editor work} (stage1 and stage2 changes, already archived):
the GLSL PBR programs and the HDR/tonemap resolve path, the whole shadow-map subsystem (cascades,
spot atlas, bias scheme, PCF, \texttt{radiant/cascade.h}), the \texttt{IShader} PBR accessors
(\texttt{include/ishaders.h:137--139}), light plumbing in the entity plugin, the benchmark map, and
the units and shadow-constant documentation. \textbf{Retargeted:} how a material is
\emph{identified}, not how it is shaded, and which game the light entities are authored under.

\section{Compatibility contract}\label{sec:contract}

Rules every stage satisfies. ``Stock'' means ioquake3 with \texttt{cl\_renderer opengl1} or
\texttt{opengl2} and default cvars.

\begin{tabular}{@{}p{0.17\textwidth}p{0.42\textwidth}p{0.35\textwidth}@{}}
\toprule
Content & Rule & Grounds \\
\midrule
Shader text & Vanilla Quake~3 shader keywords only, plus top-level \texttt{qer\_pbr\_*}. No rend2
  keywords (\texttt{stage}, \texttt{specularReflectance}, \texttt{gloss}, \texttt{roughness},
  \texttt{normalScale}, \ldots) and no other custom keywords. &
  An unknown keyword invalidates the whole shader in both renderers (\texttt{renderergl2/tr\_shader.cpp:1267},
  \texttt{renderergl1/tr\_shader.cpp:972}); top-level \texttt{qer*}/\texttt{q3map*} are skipped by
  both, but not inside stages. \\
Materials & As §\ref{sec:materials}. & \\
rend2 siblings & No image named \texttt{<base>\_n}, \texttt{\_nh} or \texttt{\_s} next to a stage
  image. &
  rend2 loads those with no keyword (\texttt{renderergl2/tr\_shader.cpp:2150--2215}) and would

\subsection{Verification}\label{sec:verify}

The stock check (\texttt{tools/stockcheck/}) runs the fork's ioquake3 build
(\texttt{ioq3-custom/ioq3/build/Release}). Its renderers were machine-converted to C++ and are
otherwise original; each run records the fork's commit so drift is traceable. Matrix:
\texttt{cl\_renderer opengl1} and \texttt{opengl2}, default cvars. Each run uses a clean temporary
basepath and homepath holding only the stock paks and the pk3, \texttt{+set developer 1 +set
logfile 2 +devmap <map>}, then for each documented view (\texttt{maps/<map>.cameras})
\texttt{setviewpos}, a settle wait, \texttt{viewpos} (the reported eye position is recorded in
\texttt{views.txt}, because \texttt{setviewpos} pushes the player forward) and
\texttt{screenshot}. It fails on any \texttt{WARNING} line or \texttt{<classname> doesn't have a
spawn function} line not on a reviewed allowlist (which starts with the
\texttt{light\_spot}/\texttt{light\_sun}/\texttt{light\_area} spawn-function lines), on \texttt{R\_FindImageFile could
not find}, and on \texttt{Couldn't find image file for shader} (\texttt{developer} only,
\texttt{tr\_shader.cpp:3336}). A missing image is excused only by an allowlist entry naming the
exact line of an image the engine replaces with a built-in fallback (\texttt{renderergl2}'s
\texttt{gfx/2d/sunflare}). It also fails on a missing screenshot, on a \texttt{viewpos} that is
missing or more than 256 units from its request (an ignored teleport), on an abnormal exit and on
a timeout. The compile (\texttt{-meta -keeplights}, \texttt{-vis -saveprt}, \texttt{-light
-shbake} with both bakes, which needs q3map2 built with \texttt{SH\_BAKER=1}) fails on a leak, on
q3map2's \texttt{Unknown q3map\_* directive} and when the light stage leaves no side file at
\texttt{maps/<map>/<map>.shsf}; a side file left in the source mod is deleted first, and the pk3
carries the new one. The engine only parses shaders a map uses, so the check covers exactly the
shaders the map references. The enhanced check is the same run with \texttt{cl\_renderer sh}
(added by C2). It must also assert from the console that \texttt{renderer\_sh} loaded, because a
module that fails to load prints \texttt{failed:} (\texttt{cl\_main.c:3190}) and the engine
continues on \texttt{opengl2}.
There is no calibration mode: nothing is matched against q3map2's lightmap.

\subsection{Open decisions}\label{sec:open}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Game code.} baseq3 needs nothing built but has no flashlight on any target. A small
  mod's cgame can attach a light to the player with \texttt{trap\_R\_AddLightToScene} on every
  engine, stock included; that call is omni-only (\texttt{tr\_public.h:75}: origin, intensity,
  colour), so a spot flashlight needs a \texttt{refexport\_t} extension that only the customised
  engine has, with the omni light as the stock fallback. The BSP and pk3 rules do not depend on the
  choice. It gates the flashlight of Stage~1 (A10) and the spot-flashlight ABI (C8, 3.6).
\end{itemize}
The parser for \texttt{renderer\_sh}, open until revision 7, is decided: it writes its own
(§\ref{sec:rev8}).

\section{Material authoring}\label{sec:materials}

A PBR material is an ordinary lightmapped Quake~3 shader, written in \texttt{scripts/*.shader} in
the editor's Shader Editor or any text editor. Its stages are vanilla; all PBR data is in top-level
\texttt{qer\_pbr\_*} keywords, which every stock renderer skips:

\begin{verbatim}
textures/town/brick
{
    qer_editorimage textures/town/brick_c
    qer_pbr_normal textures/town/brick_nrm             // tangent space, linear
    qer_pbr_metallicRoughness textures/town/brick_mr   // G = roughness, B = metallic
    qer_pbr_occlusion textures/town/brick_ao           // R = occlusion
    qer_pbr_roughnessFactor 0.8
    q3map_lightmapSampleSize 8
    {
        map $lightmap
        rgbGen identity
    }
    {
        map textures/town/brick_c          // base colour
        blendFunc GL_DST_COLOR GL_ZERO
        rgbGen identity
    }
}
\end{verbatim}

\begin{tabular}{@{}p{0.30\textwidth}p{0.42\textwidth}p{0.22\textwidth}@{}}
\toprule
Property & Written as & \texttt{.mtr} equivalent \\
\midrule
Base colour (sRGB) & The image of the stage the lightmap multiplies. Stages are written
  explicitly. & \texttt{basecolor} \\
Normal (linear) & \texttt{qer\_pbr\_normal <image>}: tangent space, OpenGL Y-up. & \texttt{normal} \\
Metallic--roughness (linear) & \texttt{qer\_pbr\_metallicRoughness <image>}: G roughness,
  B metallic. Without it, metallic is \texttt{qer\_pbr\_metallicFactor} (default 0) and roughness
  is \texttt{qer\_pbr\_roughnessFactor} (default 1). & \texttt{metallicroughness} \\
Occlusion (linear) & \texttt{qer\_pbr\_occlusion <image>}: R occlusion. & \texttt{occlusion} \\
Factors & \texttt{qer\_pbr\_roughnessFactor <f>}, \texttt{qer\_pbr\_metallicFactor <f>},
  \texttt{qer\_pbr\_baseColorFactor <r> <g> <b>}. With a metallic--roughness map they default to
  1 and multiply it, as in glTF. &
  \texttt{*factor} \\
Emissive (sRGB) & An additive stage (\texttt{blendFunc add}) carrying the emission image, optional
  \texttt{rgbGen const}. \texttt{qer\_pbr\_emissiveStrength <f>} (default 1) scales it: radiance
  $=$ texel $\times$ colour $\times$ strength, in the light passes' linear units with no Quake~3
  specific scale. Stock renderers draw
  the stage unscaled. &
  \texttt{emissive} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Why emissive is a stage.} An additive stage is how Quake~3 already draws glow, so the
  stock target shows it unchanged; \texttt{sh-baker} already bakes additive layers as area lights
  (``Bake additive SH\_material\_layers surfaces as area lights'', \texttt{843237b}), so the glow
  also reaches the lightmap once 4.3 lands; \texttt{q3map\_surfacelight} is not needed.
\item \textbf{Sky emission} (revision 8). A sky shader (\texttt{surfaceparm sky}) with
  \texttt{qer\_pbr\_emissiveStrength} lights the map from its image, read as radiance the same
  way; without the keyword it emits nothing. §\ref{sec:sky}.
\item \textbf{Factors are enhanced-only.} The stock target never sees \texttt{qer\_pbr\_baseColorFactor};
  where the stock look matters, bake the factor into the texture instead.
\item \textbf{Reserved suffixes.} Never name an image \texttt{<base>\_n}, \texttt{<base>\_nh} or
  \texttt{<base>\_s}, where \texttt{<base>} is a stage image: rend2 picks those up with no keyword
  (\texttt{renderergl2/tr\_shader.cpp:2150--2215}). The example above uses \texttt{\_nrm},
  \texttt{\_mr} and \texttt{\_ao}.
\item \textbf{No rend2 stage keywords.} \texttt{stage normalMap}, \texttt{stage specularMap},
  \texttt{roughness} and the rest are rend2-only and make \texttt{renderergl1} reject the shader.
\item \textbf{Non-PBR shaders.} A lightmapped opaque shader without \texttt{qer\_pbr\_*} keywords
  is shaded in the editor preview (and in \texttt{renderer\_sh}) as a fully rough dielectric with
  its base colour, and \texttt{sh-baker} bakes it the same way.
\item \textbf{Editor support} is Stage~2.2: the Quake~3 shader parser reads all of the above and
  fills the existing \texttt{IShader} PBR accessors, the Shader Editor completes the new keywords,
  and the lighting preview renders the result.
\end{itemize}

\section{Current state (verified)}

\subsection{The engine: ioquake3, renderer modules now C++}\label{sec:rend2facts}

\begin{itemize}
\item The renderer is a runtime-loaded module: \texttt{code/client/cl\_main.c:3184} registers
  \texttt{cl\_renderer} (default \texttt{opengl2}, \texttt{CVAR\_ARCHIVE|CVAR\_LATCH}), builds
  \texttt{renderer\_<name>} and resolves \texttt{GetRefAPI} (\texttt{:3204}). \texttt{renderergl1}
  and \texttt{renderergl2} ship side by side; a new backend is a sibling, not a replacement.
\item \textbf{Both renderer modules are now C++} (\texttt{0135354f}, ``CPP Renderers''), and
  \texttt{tr\_public.h} already carries \texttt{extern "C"} guards (\texttt{:25}, \texttt{:214}).
  The \texttt{refexport\_t}/\texttt{refimport\_t} boundary is therefore usable from C++ directly.
  \textbf{This obsoletes the separate C-ABI wrapper} previously planned for \texttt{sh-renderer}
  (\texttt{ioq3-custom/docs/c\_interface\_design.tex}); only the control inversion --- create the
  context through the engine's \texttt{GLimp\_Init}, expose discrete load/update/render/resize/shutdown
  calls, load the GL functions through SDL --- remains necessary.
\item \textbf{There is no existing GL context to take over.} Each renderer module calls
  \texttt{GLimp\_Init} itself: \texttt{code/sdl/sdl\_glimp.c} is compiled into every module
  (\texttt{cmake/renderer\_common.cmake:15--18}; \texttt{renderergl2/tr\_init.cpp:264--267}), and
  it asks for at most GL~3.2 core (\texttt{sdl\_glimp.c:545--548}). sh-renderer needs 4.6 core.
  3.1 has the decision.
\item \textbf{\texttt{ri.FS\_FileExists} searches no pk3.} It is
  \texttt{FS\_FileExists\_HomeData} (\texttt{cl\_main.c:3238}), so a renderer probing a packed
  asset with it never finds one --- the trap that sank B6 (below). Packed assets are probed with
  \texttt{ri.FS\_ReadFile}.
\item \texttt{renderergl2} (rend2) already implements, off or on by cvar in
  \texttt{tr\_init.cpp}: \texttt{r\_pbr} (\texttt{:1241}, default \textbf{0}), \texttt{r\_hdr}
  (\texttt{:1201}, default 1), \texttt{r\_toneMap} (\texttt{:1206}), \texttt{r\_normalMapping} and
  \texttt{r\_specularMapping} (\texttt{:1225--1227}, both default 1), \texttt{r\_deluxeMapping}
  (\texttt{:1229}), \texttt{r\_cubeMapping} (\texttt{:1236}), \texttt{r\_sunlightMode}
  (\texttt{:1271}, cascaded sun shadows), \texttt{r\_dlightMode} (\texttt{:1252}; $\geq 2$ gives
  per-pixel dynamic lights \emph{with} projected shadows), plus SSAO and a tonemap post chain.
\item Under \texttt{USE\_PBR}, \texttt{glsl/lightall\_fp.glsl:398--404} is metallic--roughness with
  $F_0 = \mathrm{mix}(0.04, \text{basecolor}, \text{metallic})$, Disney diffuse and GGX-ish
  specular. Maps come from the stage keywords \texttt{normalMap} (\texttt{tr\_shader.cpp:881}) and
  \texttt{specularMap} (\texttt{:894}), the \texttt{roughness} keyword (\texttt{:976}), or the
  \texttt{\_n}/\texttt{\_nh}/\texttt{\_s} siblings it loads without a keyword (\texttt{:2150--2215}).
\item \textbf{Specular map interpretation depends on client cvars.} With \texttt{r\_pbr 0} (default)
  it is RGB reflectance plus alpha gloss; with \texttt{r\_pbr 1}, red is gloss under
  \texttt{r\_glossType} (default 1, smoothness) and green is metallic. Colour space differs too:
  \texttt{lightall\_fp.glsl:394} uses \texttt{diffuse.rgb *= diffuse.rgb}, a gamma-2.0
  approximation, where the editor uses the exact piecewise sRGB curve.
\item \textbf{rend2 loads external HDR lightmaps}: \texttt{maps/<name>/lm\_\%04d.hdr},
  promoting the lightmap atlas to \texttt{GL\_RGBA16} when the first one exists and \texttt{r\_hdr}
  is on (\texttt{tr\_bsp.cpp:279--326}). That promotion only happens when \texttt{lm\_0000.hdr}
  is a loose file in the homepath game directory: \texttt{ri.FS\_FileExists} is
  \texttt{FS\_FileExists\_HomeData}, which searches no pk3 (\texttt{cl\_main.c:3238},
  \texttt{files.c:642--653}). Its size check also counts the header, so only a 196608-byte file
  with a 3-byte header passes, and the page's last texel is then read past the buffer
  (\texttt{tr\_bsp.cpp:336--381}). \texttt{vertlight.raw} (12 bytes per draw vertex) and
  \texttt{lightgrid.raw} (ambient and directed RGB per grid point) do load from a pk3 at
  \texttt{r\_hdr 1}, in the BSP bytes' units (\texttt{R\_ColorShiftLightingFloats}, 2/255 at
  defaults), and rend2 prints nothing on success.
\item \textbf{Decided (2026-11-20): B6 is dropped.} B6 planned to write those three files from
  the full bake, for precision on \texttt{opengl2}, the default stock renderer. A shipped pk3 can
  never use \texttt{lm\_*.hdr}, so the lightmap, the bulk of the light, would stay in BSP bytes on
  \texttt{opengl2} anyway; \texttt{opengl2} applies no sRGB encode, so the vertex and grid files
  would have to carry B5's encoding as floats rather than linear light to match it; and what is left,
  sub-byte precision for vertex-lit surfaces and the grid, isn't worth a second output path.
  \texttt{opengl2} keeps reading the BSP bytes everywhere.
\item \textbf{None of rend2's PBR, specular, normal-map or HDR-lightmap features are used}
  (revision 5). They are listed here because the contract must keep rend2 from picking them up by
  accident.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{The prior attempt}\label{sec:prior}

\texttt{ioq3-map-exporter} (\texttt{.pk3} $\to$ IBSP $\to$ glTF), \texttt{ioq3-materialize}
(Real-ESRGAN upscale, DeepBump normals, ORM packing), \texttt{sh-baker} and \texttt{sh-renderer}
were built to enhance existing levels such as \texttt{q3dm1} and \texttt{mpteam3}. How the bake was
wired, which is why it does not carry over as-is:
\begin{itemize}
\item The exporter writes each vertex's q3map2 lightmap coordinates, which are local to one
  lightmap page, as \texttt{TEXCOORD\_1}.
\item \texttt{sh-baker} ignores them: its loader reads only \texttt{TEXCOORD\_0}
  (\texttt{src/loader.cpp:170}, \texttt{:212}). It builds a fresh atlas with xatlas, splitting
  vertices along chart seams, and always builds a single atlas, whose size xatlas picks near the
  requested width (\texttt{src/atlas.cpp:110--207}); \texttt{--skip\_parameterization} still repacks the charts.
  It writes a new glTF with its own \texttt{TEXCOORD\_1} (\texttt{src/saver.cpp:769}).
\item \texttt{sh-renderer} renders that glTF (\texttt{src/loader.cpp:142}, \texttt{:941}), never
  the BSP.
\end{itemize}
\textbf{Carried over:} \texttt{sh-renderer} as \texttt{renderer\_sh} (Stage~3) and \texttt{sh-baker}
as a library linked into q3map2 (Stage~4). \texttt{ioq3-materialize} and the exporter are not part
of this plan, and neither is its parsing code: \texttt{renderer\_sh} writes its own loader
(§\ref{sec:rev8}). The glTF path is dropped from \texttt{sh-renderer}, not kept for its own
tests: C0 deletes the glTF loader, tinygltf, tinyexr and the EXR/xatlas SH atlas input
(§\ref{sec:rev8}).

\subsection{2.1 Decision: \texttt{pbr.game} is frozen}

gives both stock shaders and the preview:
\begin{itemize}
\item \texttt{shaders="quake3"} clears \texttt{g\_pbrGame} (\texttt{renderstate.cpp:2202}), and
  with it the PBR shading path (\texttt{:3054}) and both shadow atlases ---

\section{Stage 3: \texttt{renderer\_sh} and engine-side parity}

\textbf{Outcome.} A second renderer module that carries the project, then direct PBR and shadows
matching the editor preview.

\textbf{Shape (revision 8).} \texttt{renderer\_sh} is \texttt{ioq3-custom/sh-renderer} grown into
an ioquake3 renderer module, loaded by \texttt{cl\_renderer sh} through \texttt{GetRefAPI}; the
engine glue lives in \texttt{ioq3/code/renderersh/} and \texttt{cmake/renderer\_sh.cmake}. It is
invoked directly by the engine only; the glTF path is gone (§\ref{sec:rev8}). Changes to
sh-renderer go as pull requests to \texttt{github.com/DaviesX/sh-renderer}, reviewed and merged by
the user. It works in raw Z-up map units, as the editor and q3map2 do. Packed assets are probed with
\texttt{ri.FS\_ReadFile}, never \texttt{ri.FS\_FileExists}, which searches no pk3
(§\ref{sec:rend2facts}).

Tasks (Track~C): C0 cleanup and C1 module bring-up (3.1); C2 Tier~1 (3.3); C3a sky and fog, C3b the
rest of Tier~2 (3.3); C4 direct-light parity (3.4); C4b the side-file loader (4.4); C5 shadow parity
(3.5); C6 SH at runtime (4.4); C7 LTC area lights (4.5); C8 the spot-flashlight ABI (3.6). A task
may run out of order once its \texttt{needs} are met.

\subsection{3.1 Decision: build the Quake 3 feature set out in sh-renderer}

\textbf{Decided.} The missing feature set is added to \texttt{sh-renderer}, which becomes
\texttt{renderer\_sh}. \texttt{renderergl2} is left untouched. Rejected: modifying rend2 to host
sh-renderer's SH reconstruction and Forward+ culling.

Rationale:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{The work is additive.} Nearly every item in the inventory below is a new module in a
  codebase whose architecture is already written down and enforced. Hosting SH in rend2 would
  instead mean editing its shader-permutation system, its backend command buffer and its
  \texttt{tess} batching --- where its accumulated complexity lives, and what is hardest to reason
  about from outside.
\item \textbf{The two renderers coexist, so an incomplete one costs nothing.}
  \texttt{cl\_renderer} is a latched cvar: \texttt{renderer\_sh} can lack sky, marks and
  cinematics and still be useful for walking the town and judging light, with \texttt{opengl2} one
  cvar away as an A/B reference on the same map. Modifying rend2 has the opposite property --- a
  half-finished integration degrades a renderer that works today, and that renderer is the stock
  target.
\item \textbf{The architectural directives survive.} GL 4.6, DSA-only, Forward+, SSBOs stay intact
  rather than being recorded as superseded.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{What changes in sh-renderer's inputs.} It renders the BSP, not a glTF: draw surfaces with
their q3map2 lightmap coordinates, materials from the shader text of §\ref{sec:materials}, the SH
bands from the side file of Stage~4. It reads them with its own small BSP and shader loader over
\texttt{ri.FS\_*}, with \texttt{renderergl2}'s \texttt{tr\_bsp}, \texttt{tr\_shader} and
\texttt{tr\_curve} as the reference for meaning; it does not link \texttt{libioq3\_map}. No glTF
loader is kept, not even for its tests. The Q3 layer compositor already on sh-renderer's main
(\texttt{q3\_layer.h}, \texttt{q3\_layer\_bind}, \texttt{q3\_composite.glsl}, per-layer
descriptor SSBOs) is reused, fed from shader text instead of glTF extras.

\textbf{C0: cleanup for the module} (one sh-renderer PR). Delete the glTF and EXR path and its
dependencies as listed in §\ref{sec:rev8}, rewrite \texttt{GEMINI.md}'s phases to match C1--C7 and
the module stack (no GLFW, gflags, glog, tinygltf or tinyexr), and switch the tests to synthetic
inputs.

\textbf{C1: the one part that is not additive}, planned as a refactor rather than a feature:
\texttt{sh-renderer} currently owns its process. It uses \texttt{glog}, \texttt{gflags}, its own
file I/O, and its own GLFW window and frame loop. As a renderer module:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Build.} \texttt{cmake/renderer\_sh.cmake} behind \texttt{BUILD\_RENDERER\_SH}.
  \texttt{sh\_renderer} is a shared library built with \texttt{-march=native} today
  (\texttt{sh-renderer/CMakeLists.txt:11}, \texttt{:114}); the module links it as a static or
  object library, and \texttt{-march=native} and the Eigen alignment it implies stay out of the
  engine's translation units.
\item \textbf{GL context.} \texttt{GLimp\_Init} in the shared \texttt{sdl\_glimp.c} gains a
  context-request parameter that only \texttt{renderer\_sh} sets: 4.6 core, debug, sRGB
  framebuffer. \texttt{opengl1} and \texttt{opengl2} request exactly what they do today. Below 4.6,
  \texttt{renderer\_sh} stops with \texttt{ri.Error}. glad is loaded through
  \texttt{SDL\_GL\_GetProcAddress} after \texttt{GLimp\_Init}, in translation units separate from
  \texttt{sdl\_glimp.c}'s \texttt{qgl*} pointers.
\item \textbf{Window, input, frame.} GLFW and \texttt{input.cpp} are removed; input belongs to the
  engine. The frame loop is inverted onto \texttt{BeginFrame}, \texttt{RenderScene} and
  \texttt{EndFrame}.
\item \textbf{Engine services.} Logging through a shim over \texttt{ri.Printf}/\texttt{ri.Error},
  configuration through \texttt{ri.Cvar\_Get}, GLSL and \emph{every} other file through
  \texttt{ri.FS\_ReadFile}, so assets come from the pk3 through the engine's VFS. Level-lifetime
  buffers may use \texttt{ri.Hunk\_Alloc}; the rest \texttt{ri.Malloc} or the C++ allocator.
\item \textbf{Gamma.} ioquake3's \texttt{r\_gamma} and hardware gamma are reconciled with
  \texttt{GL\_FRAMEBUFFER\_SRGB}.
\item \textbf{Exit.} A cleared frame under \texttt{cl\_renderer sh}, with the console proving
  that \texttt{renderer\_sh} loaded (a failed load falls back to \texttt{opengl2},
  §\ref{sec:verify}).
\end{itemize}
This touches every module's edges. Do it first, before any feature work, so no feature is ported
twice.

\subsection{3.4 Direct light parity}
C4; needs Tier~1 (C2) and the light entities under \texttt{Q3.game} (A6).
\begin{itemize}
\item Lights come from the BSP entity lump, preserved by \texttt{-keeplights}, read with the
  editor's physical model (§\ref{sec:units}) and the gamepack README's formulas: \texttt{light},
  \texttt{light\_spot}, \texttt{light\_sun}, \texttt{light\_area} (from 4.5) and their keys,
  with no conversion constant --- radiant intensity flux$/4\pi$ for points and
  flux$/(2\pi(1-\cos\texttt{cone}))$ for spots, normalised \texttt{\_color}, zero beyond
  \texttt{radius}. Light units are not open: the editor's model is authoritative. sh-renderer's
  glTF light scale (\texttt{kLightIntensityScale}) and \texttt{ComputeLightRadius} are deleted.
\item The sky term leaves the direct-light pass. sh-renderer adds \texttt{u\_sky\_color}
  $\times$ sun intensity$/10$ $\times$ baked visibility (\texttt{radiance.frag:423--424}), a flat
  colour. C6 replaces it with the sky shader's SH irradiance times the same visibility
  (§\ref{sec:sky}); 3.4's parity check runs with sky emission off.
\item Every editor light --- point, spot and sun --- is evaluated at runtime, with the per-light
  caster rule (point lights never cast; spots and suns unless \texttt{\_shadows 0}). \texttt{renderer\_sh} never
  reads baked direct light: the side file holds only the indirect bake (4.3). The editor can't read
  the side file and the engine can, so direct light stays where the preview computes it.
\item Shadow atlas tiles: the editor gives its 16 tiles to the 16 lights nearest the camera;
  sh-renderer ranks by flux$/d^2$ and caps at 10 (\texttt{scene.cpp:732--747}). The engine matches
  the editor.
\item Emissive surfaces give direct light at runtime only through an attached \texttt{light\_area}
  or \texttt{\_proxy} light, which arrives with 4.5; until then they only glow.
\item Exit criterion: the gamepack README's unshadowed parity check --- the editor lighting preview
  and the engine's direct-light-only render from the documented camera, with ambient and sky
  emission off, agree to a mean absolute error of at most 2/255 in linear light, with no
  $8\times8$ block above 6/255. 4.5 extends the same check to the benchmark fireplace's
  \texttt{light\_area}.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{3.5 Shadow parity and solid hulls}
C5; needs C4, the side-file loader C4b (4.4) and B5.
\begin{itemize}
\item Take the solid hull mesh that \texttt{-shbake} writes into the side file (4.3), through the
  side-file loader (C4b, 4.4): one closed
  convex hull per casting brush, structural and detail, which q3map2 builds from the brush and
  brushside lumps. Use the hulls as front-face-culled casters; draw surfaces remain receivers. The
  bake's blockers and the renderer's casters are then one solid set, and nothing is rebuilt at
  load. No BSP format change: the lumps already keep every plane of every solid brush, including
  void-facing ones.
\item Alpha-tested draw surfaces cast too: surfaces whose shader the editor's caster rule says casts
  through its alpha (the fence, leaf cards; \texttt{trans} with \texttt{alphashadow}) are drawn into the
  shadow maps with their alpha test, alongside the hulls. They are non-solid, so the hulls alone
  would drop them, and the editor and sh-baker already cast them. Hulls plus these surfaces replace
  sh-renderer's ``every non-additive surface casts''. Cascade range and padding are in map units,
  matched to the README.
\item The bake does not follow the caster rule's per-light part: it shadows every light
  (§\ref{sec:units}). That changes only baked light (the stock lightmap and, slightly, bounced
  light), not the runtime shadows compared here.
\item Known gap: other brushes with non-solid shaders are dropped from the collision lumps. If such a
  brush must cast, mark its material to keep it, or emit casters separately.
\item Reconcile against the editor's scheme, which deliberately uses \emph{first}-depth casters
  with normal-offset and slope-scaled bias rather than second depth, because second depth is
  undefined for patches and models. The constants and their provenance are already tabulated in
  the gamepack README; that table moves with the pack in Stage 2.
\item Exit criterion: the town block's shadows match between editor and engine.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{3.6 Spot-flashlight ABI}

\begin{itemize}
\item \texttt{trap\_R\_AddLightToScene} is omni-only. A \texttt{refexport\_t} extension adds a

\item A mod's cgame must detect the extension before calling it, because an unknown syscall is

\item The ABI's shape --- a \texttt{REF\_API\_VERSION} bump or an extension lookup --- is decided
  in the task.
\end{itemize}

\section{Stage 4: SH indirect, baked by q3map2}

\textbf{Outcome.} \texttt{sh-baker}, inside q3map2, bakes the editor's lights on q3map2's own
lightmap pages in two bakes over the same points: indirect SH bands for \texttt{renderer\_sh}, and
full irradiance for the stock lightmap, light grid and vertex colours of the lvlworld build. No BSP
format change.

\subsection{4.1 Decision: link \texttt{sh-baker} into q3map2}

\textbf{Decided.} q3map2 links \texttt{sh-baker}'s shared library and bakes SH on its own luxels
inside \texttt{-light} (\texttt{-light -shbake}), in place of \texttt{-light}'s lighting math. Rejected:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Exporter, glTF, xatlas atlas} (the prior pipeline). The bake lives in a separate
  atlas over a separate, vertex-split mesh; nothing maps it back to the BSP, so
  \texttt{renderer\_sh} would have to render a second geometry and keep fog, shader stages, PVS
  and patches in step with it.
\item \textbf{Writing xatlas coordinates back into the BSP.} Needs the draw surfaces rewritten to
  hold split vertices and patches converted to triangles (\texttt{-patchmeta}) --- the largest
  option.
\end{itemize}
Grounds: q3map2 already has the BSP, shader parsing, patch tessellation, image loading, the light
entities, lightmap page allocation and light-grid points. The stage passes the editor's light
entities to \texttt{sh-baker} unchanged, so units agree by construction (§\ref{sec:units}). Baking
in q3map2's lightmap space means \texttt{renderer\_sh} uses the BSP's own coordinates with no
mapping file, and a second bake over the same points fills the stock lightmap (revision 6, 4.3).

\subsection{4.2 \texttt{sh-baker} changes}

Delivered as pull requests to \texttt{sh-baker}, reviewed and merged by its maintainer. Each keeps
the CLI's current behaviour and its tests passing. There is no separate library boundary:
q3map2 includes the headers in \texttt{src/} directly and links the \texttt{libsh\_baker.so} that
\texttt{sh-baker}'s own CMake builds (4.3), so there is no separate public header.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{q3map2 input contract} (B2, openspec change \texttt{sh-baker-q3map2-input}, shipped as
  a stack of seven PRs). \emph{Done (\texttt{sh-baker} PRs \#54--\#60).}
  \begin{itemize}
  \item An owned material-layer model: the Quake~3 stage stack becomes \texttt{sh-baker} types in
    \texttt{material.h}, which the glTF loader and saver translate to and from
    \texttt{SH\_material\_layers}. No tinygltf in the headers q3map2 includes.
  \item A q3map2 loader translation unit (\texttt{loader\_q3map2.\{h,cpp\}}) that turns one draw
    surface into checked tracing geometry: winding flipped to counter-clockwise, malformed input
    stopped by a \texttt{CHECK}, zero normals repaired, degenerate triangles dropped, and tangents
    from the MikkTSpace code it shares with the glTF loader. Material-less surfaces block light
    without shading.
  \item Baking caller-supplied points: \texttt{BakeSHLightMap} bakes any list of points, laid out
    $N\times1$, in one call with one BVH. This is how q3map2's luxels are baked (4.3).
  \end{itemize}
  This replaces the multi-page UV layouts of earlier drafts. xatlas, as \texttt{sh-baker} calls it,
  never emits several atlases (with \texttt{texelsPerUnit} 0 it grows one), \texttt{sh-baker}'s
  rasterizer snaps to whole texels while q3map2 puts lightmap coordinates on luxel centres, and
  q3map2 already owns the luxel mapping. So \texttt{sh-baker} rasterizes nothing for q3map2.
\item \textbf{Alpha in shadow rays} (B2b). NEE's visibility test treats any hit as opaque
  (\texttt{light.cpp:265}, \texttt{:339}), so fences and leaf cards block direct light completely.
  The shadow rays honour alpha the way the tracer's indirect rays already do, so they cast through
  their cut-outs. Kept out of item~1 because it changes bake output; needed by 4.3.
  \emph{Done (\texttt{sh-baker} PR \#62).}
\item \textbf{Progress and cancel.} One \texttt{BakeSHLightMap} call already builds the Embree
  device, the BVH and the light trees once for all its points (\texttt{baker.cpp:95--96}); it
  gains progress and cancel callbacks, plus a done callback on the calling thread.
  \emph{Done (\texttt{sh-baker} PR \#63, then \texttt{ce5419a}).}
\item \textbf{Outputs.} \texttt{sh-baker} returns SH, irradiance and environment visibility per
  luxel; q3map2 assembles the pages, the light grid and the vertex colours. No rend2 layout. The
  SH side-file format (L0, L1, L2 and visibility per page, plus the solid hull mesh of 3.5) is not
  an \texttt{sh-baker} change after all: q3map2 produces everything the file holds, so the format
  lives in q3map2 as the \emph{q3map2 extension} (\texttt{q3map2\_extension.\{h,cpp\}}), with an
  optional Radiance \texttt{.hdr} dump of L0 for inspection. \texttt{sh-baker} stays unchanged.
  \emph{Done (q3map2; \texttt{sh-baker} PR \#64 closed unmerged).}
\item \textbf{The editor's light model} (B5). \texttt{Light::range}, which the light tree honours
  (\texttt{LightBounds::max\_range} and a per-point choice between the BVH and the directional
  set), and \texttt{Light::ConeFalloff::kSmoothstep}, a hard edge for equal cones. A per-light
  shadow switch and an exact, separate punctual-direct result were rejected in review: each point's
  \texttt{SHCoeffs::irradiance} (\texttt{sh\_coeffs.h:26}) is already a cosine-weighted estimate of
  its irradiance, a per-light pass costs time that light-tree sampling spends better with hundreds
  of lights, and order-2 SH smears direct area light. So every light casts in a bake.
  \emph{Done (\texttt{sh-baker} PR \#65, \texttt{ad8f39f}).}
\item \textbf{Indirect-only skips all direct light} (B5). \texttt{BakeConfig::indirect\_only} drops
  punctual \emph{and} area direct light at the baked point; bounces still see every light.
  \emph{Done (\texttt{sh-baker} PR \#66, \texttt{046ff2e}).}
\item \textbf{Full-sphere points} (B5). \texttt{SurfacePoint::full\_sphere}, default false: rays
  uniform over the sphere weighted $4\pi$, next-event estimation with a zero normal and no
  back-face rejection at the baked point only. One such point per light-grid point replaces the
  pair of opposed hemispheres. Defaults keep every existing bake bit-identical.
  \emph{Done (\texttt{sh-baker} PR \#67, \texttt{fdad058}).}
\item \textbf{Fixed sample count} (B5). \texttt{BakeConfig::confidence\_threshold} at or below 0
  turns off the sensor's adaptive stop, so every valid point takes exactly \texttt{samples} rays.
  Before, the stop's absolute floor ended any point whose per-sample L0 was constant (one lit only
  by unshadowed point lights) at its 64-sample minimum, whatever \texttt{samples} said. The default
  0.01 is unchanged. \emph{Done (\texttt{sh-baker} PR \#68, \texttt{8f181ba}).}
\item \textbf{Sky radiance in the full bake} (B11, revision 8). When
  \texttt{Scene::environment} (\texttt{std::optional<Environment>}, \texttt{scene.h:89--114},
  \texttt{:145--146}) is set and the bake is not \texttt{indirect\_only}, rays that escape, at the
  baked point and after bounces, return its radiance. \texttt{indirect\_only} bakes never add sky
  radiance; they keep recording only the escape fraction (environment visibility,
  \texttt{baker.cpp:202,231}), which is what lets the runtime change the sky. An unset environment
  gives bit-identical bakes. Coordinated with B7, whose guided sensor rays already include sky
  escapes. Not started.
\end{enumerate}
The existing light types already cover what q3map2 passes in: point, spot, directional and area
lights; material-less geometry is already a pure occluder. Today the point bake never reads the
scene's environment (a ray that misses returns zero), so there is no sky light yet, and outdoor
light comes from \texttt{light\_sun}. Item~9 adds it to the full bake only: sky surfaces still
never cast, and the indirect bake stays sky-free apart from visibility (§\ref{sec:sky}).

\subsection{4.3 \texttt{-shbake} in q3map2}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Placement.} \texttt{-shbake} runs inside \texttt{-light}
  (\texttt{q3map2 -light -shbake}), after \texttt{MapRawLightmap} has mapped every raw lightmap's
  luxels (origins, normals, clusters). It replaces \texttt{IlluminateRawLightmap}'s lighting math,
  and the light-grid and vertex-light passes, with \texttt{sh-baker}. \texttt{StoreSurfaceLightmaps}
  then packs the result. It cannot run on the finished BSP: the final pages exist only after
  \texttt{StoreSurfaceLightmaps}. Stock q3map2 packs by lit values; under \texttt{-shbake} collapse
  and solid stamping are skipped and \texttt{-approx} is refused, so the page layout does not depend
  on lit bytes and is the same in every toggle mode.
\item \textbf{Scene.} Draw surfaces --- planar, meta, triangle soups and tessellated patches ---
  go through \texttt{sh-baker}'s q3map2 loader as receivers. Materials from q3map2's
  shader info: the base-colour image, the \texttt{qer\_pbr\_*} maps and factors (q3map2's shader
  parser learns them), additive stages as emitters. Lights are the editor's \texttt{light},
  \texttt{light\_spot} and \texttt{light\_sun} entities read with the physical model of
  §\ref{sec:units}, not q3map2's light parsing. With B12 the map's sky shader becomes the full
  bake's \texttt{Environment}, derived as in the editor; the indirect bake gets none
  (§\ref{sec:sky}). Occluding surfaces follow the editor's
  shadow-caster rule (§\ref{sec:rev6}). Every light casts: \texttt{sh-baker} has no per-light shadow
  switch, so point lights and \texttt{\_shadows 0} lights are shadowed too (§\ref{sec:units}).
\item \textbf{Solid hulls.} q3map2's draw surfaces are thin, and rays can slip through the cracks
  between them. The blockers are one closed hull per casting brush, structural and detail, built
  from the BSP's brush lumps with q3map2's winding code (\texttt{BaseWindingForPlane},
  \texttt{ChopWindingInPlace}). Each drawn plane is pushed inward by $\varepsilon$, a little more
  than the sensor's \texttt{tnear}, so the visible face is always hit first; \texttt{nodraw}
  (caulk) planes stay in place, so caulk seams leave no gap; bevel sides are skipped;
  each hull is added as a material-less surface, which blocks light without shading. The draw
  surfaces stay the receivers and own the luxels. Brushless surfaces (patches, \texttt{misc\_model}
  props, foliage) stay thin. The un-shrunk hulls go into the side file for 3.5.
\item \textbf{Points.} One list: every mapped super-luxel, one full-sphere point per light-grid
  point (4.2 item~7), and every draw vertex. Both bakes take the same list, so their results align
  index for index; \texttt{sh-baker} seeds per index, so each bake is deterministic.
\item \textbf{Two independent bakes.} Each is an ordinary \texttt{BakeSHLightMap} call with one BVH
  for the map, and each converges on its own.
  \begin{itemize}
  \item The \emph{full bake} (\texttt{indirect\_only} false) feeds the stock target. Lightmap and
    vertex irradiance is the point's \texttt{SHCoeffs::irradiance}, a cosine-weighted Monte Carlo
    estimate at its normal, with no SH ringing; \texttt{-super N} averages away its noise. Each
    grid point's full-sphere SH is fit as ambient plus one directional light: the direction is the
    luminance-weighted L1, directed is the $E_\perp$ of a delta light along it, ambient comes from
    what L0 has left. The bytes then follow the engine's model lighting. Lightmap, grid and vertex
    colours thus carry direct plus indirect light, as \texttt{renderer\_sh} shows it.
  \item The \emph{indirect bake} (\texttt{indirect\_only} true) skips all direct light at the
    point, punctual and emissive; bounces see every light. It feeds \texttt{renderer\_sh}: the side
    file's luxel pages and its SH grid.
  \end{itemize}
\item \textbf{Toggles.} \texttt{-shbake} runs both bakes by default, which gives the shippable pk3;
  the three Town builds and the stock check use it. \texttt{-shnofull} skips the full bake: the lightmap
  pages and vertex colours get the neutral byte 64 (a multiplier of 1.0 on \texttt{opengl1}), and
  the grid gets neutral ambient (63) with no directed light. \texttt{-shnoindirect} skips the indirect
  bake and writes no side file. Both together bake nothing. Two bakes cost about twice one.
\item \textbf{Side file.} Written to \texttt{maps/<name>/}, inside the level's single pk3, through
  the q3map2 extension's writer (4.2 item~4). It always holds the indirect bake: the luxel pages
  (side-file page $i$ is BSP lightmap page $i$; skipped luxels are filled before writing), the hull
  mesh in BSP units, and an SH grid. The SH grid has one probe per BSP light-grid point, in the
  grid's order, with nine RGB coefficients in the pages' basis and a valid flag; a probe left in
  solid after q3map2's nudge is invalid and zero. Never \texttt{lm\_*.hdr}, which stock rend2 would
  load. The irradiance-to-byte encoding is chosen here (§\ref{sec:units}).
\item \textbf{Anti-aliasing.} From \texttt{-super N}: raw lightmaps are mapped at $N\times$
  resolution, each super-luxel is baked as a point, and \texttt{StoreSurfaceLightmaps} averages
  them; SH coefficients are linear, so the side data averages the same way. \texttt{-samples}'
  adaptive subsampling is part of the replaced lighting and is not available.
\item \textbf{Units.} The sensor's ray offset (\texttt{tnear}, 0.005) is in metres, while q3map2
  works in Quake units, so the stage converts units or scales the scene.
\item \textbf{Gaps the stage reports.} q3map2-only light inputs --- \texttt{spawnflags},
  \texttt{fade}, \texttt{q3map\_sun}, \texttt{q3map\_skylight}, \texttt{q3map\_surfacelight} ---
  are not read; the stage warns on them. \texttt{light\_area} and lights with \texttt{\_proxy 1}
  are skipped without a warning, because the emitter's surface already bakes their light (revision
  7). An emissive surface with no attached light gets one warning per shader, with a count. A
  surface counts as attached when a \texttt{light\_area}'s rectangle is coplanar with it and
  overlaps it, or a \texttt{\_proxy} light lies within its bounds grown by 64 units.
\item \textbf{Encoding (as built).} A lightmap or vertex byte is
  \[ \mathrm{round}\bigl(63.75\cdot\mathrm{sRGB}(U(E/\pi)/U(11.2))\bigr), \]
  clamped to $0..64$, where $U$ is
  Hable's filmic curve ($A=0.15$, $B=0.50$, $C=0.10$, $D=0.20$, $E=0.02$, $F=0.30$) and 11.2 the
  white point; $E/\pi$ at or above it gives 64. The grid fit uses the engine's constants, checked
  against the fork's \texttt{renderergl1}: load-time shift 2, \texttt{r\_ambientScale} 0.6,
  \texttt{r\_directedScale} 1, a floor of 32 added to every model's ambient, bytes up to 63
  before the shift rescales a triple. An all-zero ambient triple out of solid is written
  1~1~1, because the engine skips a cell whose ambient is zero; directed is then solved from that
  final byte. The neutral bytes under \texttt{-shnofull} are 64 (lightmap and vertex) and ambient 63
  with directed 0 (grid). The constants live in \texttt{light\_sh\_model.h}; the gamepack README
  has the opengl2 differences (shift 1, $\times 2$ except \texttt{rgbGen vertex}, auto-exposure).
\item \textbf{Measured on the benchmark (B5).} At the defaults (128 samples per point, 5 bounces)
  on a 12-core machine, both bakes run over the same 41939 points: 38469 super-luxel slots (33510 mapped and baked),
  1275 grid points (1272 baked, 3 in solid) and 2195 vertices. The indirect bake takes 0.7\,s, the
  full bake 0.8\,s, and the whole \texttt{-light -shbake} stage about 1.5\,s; at
  \texttt{-shsamples 16} each bake took 0.1\,s (task 6.2). The output is 3 lightmap pages, a 363776-byte BSP, and a 2929891-byte side file with 86
  hulls (2088 vertices) and 1275 SH probes ($17\times25\times3$, 1272 valid). \texttt{-threads 1}
  and the default thread count give BSPs identical in every lump (only the header's compile
  time differs) and byte-identical side files. The stock check (both bakes,
  opengl1 and opengl2) passes in 25\,s with a 2322020-byte pk3 (2233284 bytes before \#68, whose exact sample
  counts change the compressed side file).
\item \textbf{Samples and quality presets.} q3map2 passes \texttt{confidence\_threshold} 0
  (4.2 item~8), so \texttt{-shsamples N} is the exact number of rays per point and a bake's time
  grows linearly with samples times points. The overlay's build menu has three Town builds with the
  same three stages; only the light stage differs. Measured on the benchmark, with the noise taken
  against a 4096-sample \texttt{-super 2} bake as the rms lightmap byte error (steps of $0..64$)
  and the rms relative error of each side-file luxel's L0 luminance:

  \smallskip
  \begin{tabular}{@{}lllll@{}}
  \toprule
  Build & Light stage & Compile & Lightmap & Side file \\
  \midrule
  draft & \texttt{-shsamples 128} & 1.6\,s & 5.0 & 119\% \\
  pre-release & \texttt{-shsamples 512 -super 2} & 22\,s & 0.75 & 22\% \\
  final & \texttt{-shsamples 2048 -super 2} & 90\,s & 0.28 & 7\% \\
  \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
  \smallskip

  The draft bakes 41939 points per bake, the other two 157346. The draft's lightmap figure also
  holds the difference between \texttt{-super 1} and \texttt{-super 2}. The final preset's 0.28 is
  at the 8-bit rounding floor ($1/\sqrt{12}\approx0.29$); its dark luxels (below byte 8) are at
  0.31, the pre-release's at 0.82. A 4096-sample \texttt{-super 2} light stage takes 187\,s. The
  stock check stays at the defaults (128 samples, \texttt{-super 1}). Hard lighting needs about
  4096 samples to converge smoothly (the day-time Sponza into two $2048^2$ pages took about 1.5
  hours), so \texttt{-shsamples 4096} remains a manual option. Path guiding and denoising
  (\texttt{sh-baker} Phases 10 and 11) attack this noise in 4.6.
\item \textbf{Known gaps.} Found while building the stage; none blocks B5.
  \begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{Shadow rule.} The bake shadows every light, point lights and \texttt{\_shadows 0}
    lights included; the preview and \texttt{renderer\_sh} keep the caster rule
    (§\ref{sec:units}). 0.3 judges the difference.
  \item \emph{Emissive direct light.} The side file holds only an emitter's bounce. Its direct
    light reaches \texttt{renderer\_sh} only through an attached light, so it is missing until
    C7's LTC area lights. The benchmark's neon sign has no attached light and warns for its 5
    surfaces; A11's fireplace has a \texttt{light\_area} on its fire face and draws no warning.
  \item \emph{Convergence on L0 alone} (fixed by \texttt{sh-baker} PR \#68). The sensor stopped on
    L0's variance after its 64-sample minimum. With point lights only, light-tree sampling makes
    every sample's L0 the same, so a probe stopped at 64 samples whatever \texttt{-shsamples} said,
    and its L1 direction was the noise of 64 light picks: a probe between two opposed equal lights
    got ambient 3 and directed 5 where the fit gives 5 and 0. With the adaptive stop off it gets
    ambient 5 and directed 1 at \texttt{-shsamples 4096}.
  \item \emph{Threads.} \texttt{-threads} doesn't limit \texttt{sh-baker}'s TBB pool; the bake
    times are the same at \texttt{-threads 1} and 12. The output doesn't depend on it.
  \item \emph{Grid floor and spacing.} The engine adds $32/255$ (12.5\%) to every model's ambient,
    so models can't go as dark as the darkest walls. With the default \texttt{gridsize 64 64 128}
    the trilinear blend takes in the row 128 units up, so a model near a light reads brighter than
    the floor under it; the README suggests a smaller $z$ for the town.
  \item \emph{Monte Carlo noise.} The draft and the stock check bake 128 samples per point; the
    pre-release and final presets above lower the noise. A9 judges the shipped preset and may
    change it.
  \item \emph{Hull seam inset.} A caulk face's bake polygon stops $1/32$ unit short of a drawn
    neighbour's plane (0.25 opened a strip; flush grazed edge-row rays), so a light within $1/32$
    unit of a drawn wall plane could still leak along it.
  \end{itemize}
\item \textbf{Observations for 0.3.} A sun and shadow check on the benchmark found no
  \texttt{-shbake} bug: the bake tracks the editor preview closely. Three differences remain, none
  of them the bake's.
  \begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{Dark albedo lifts shadows.} Vanilla draws texture times lightmap, so a shadow on a
    very dark albedo keeps less contrast than the preview's: about $2.5\times$ lighter on the
    asphalt.
  \item \emph{Narrow shadows.} A shadow narrower than the 16-unit luxel (the 8-unit tree trunk)
    can't show in the lightmap.
  \item \emph{The editor's shadow map leaks under the arch.} The preview's shadow mapping lets
    light through the benchmark's arch onto the floor; the bake doesn't. This is a preview
    limitation, not a bake error.
  \end{itemize}
\item \textbf{Build.} \texttt{sh-baker} is the \texttt{tools/sh-baker} submodule, built by its own
  CMake in Release (never Debug, which adds \texttt{-fsanitize=address}) into
  \texttt{libsh\_baker.so}; its dependencies (glog, Embree, TBB, xatlas) load through the library.
  Under an opt-in flag (\texttt{SH\_BAKER=1}) q3map2's \texttt{Makefile} adds
  \texttt{tools/sh-baker/src} and Eigen to the include path, links \texttt{-lsh\_baker} (and
  \texttt{-lembree4} if the stage calls Embree itself), and sets an rpath or installs the library
  beside q3map2; a default q3map2 build needs none of this. The headers the stage includes
  (\texttt{loader\_q3map2.h}, \texttt{baker.h}) need only \texttt{src} and Eigen, because
  \texttt{sh-baker} declares the Embree handles itself (4.2 item~1). The stage lives in a new
  \texttt{light\_sh.cpp}, the only q3map2 file that includes \texttt{sh-baker}'s headers; they
  compile cleanly under q3map2's \texttt{-fno-exceptions -fno-rtti}. That file alone is compiled
  with \texttt{-march=native}, because \texttt{sh-baker}'s Release build always uses it and it
  changes Eigen's alignment: \texttt{Geometry} (through its \texttt{Affine3f} transform) is
  32-byte aligned with AVX and 16 without, and a mismatch lets the library run aligned AVX loads on
  objects q3map2 allocated. \texttt{sh-baker} logs through glog to stderr, not through
  \texttt{Sys\_Printf}, so the editor's build monitor does not show those lines.
\item \textbf{Licence.} \texttt{sh-baker} is MIT, compatible with q3map2's GPL-2.0-or-later. Embree
  and TBB are Apache-2.0, compatible with GPLv3 but not GPLv2, so a q3map2 binary built with
  \texttt{SH\_BAKER=1} is distributed under GPLv3, which ``or any later version'' permits.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{4.4 Runtime and exit}
\begin{itemize}
\item \texttt{renderer\_sh} computes direct light at runtime for every editor light (3.4) and takes
  only indirect light from the side file. Surfaces read their luxel SH and run the indirect BRDF:
  Ramamoorthi--Hanrahan cosine convolution for diffuse, SH evaluated along the reflection vector for
  specular.
\item \textbf{Side-file loader} (C4b; needs C2 and B13). \texttt{maps/<name>/<name>.shsf} version~3
  (\texttt{q3map2\_extension.h}), read through \texttt{ri.FS\_ReadFile}. Other versions are
  refused; the pages are checked against the BSP lightmap pages. When the file is absent or does not
  match, the map falls back to the BSP lightmap. The reader is MIT, written from the format
  description, because \texttt{q3map2\_extension} is GPL-2.0-or-later and sh-renderer MIT. C5 takes
  the hull mesh and C6 the pages and grid from it.
\item \textbf{SH at runtime} (C6; needs C4b). Per-page RGB L0--L2 replaces sh-renderer's EXR atlas
  and its luma-only L1/L2 (\texttt{radiance.frag:344--373}).
\item Dynamic models (players, weapons, items) and vertex-lit surfaces read the SH grid; the side
  file has no per-vertex SH. Trilinear interpolation of the eight surrounding probes' coefficients
  is exact blending because SH is linear. Invalid probes are dropped and the remaining weights
  renormalised. The result is evaluated per pixel at the normal and clamped at 0.
\item Sky light (C6, needs C3a for the sky images). At load the sky shader is projected to L0--L2
  SH with the editor's derivation; a surface adds that SH's irradiance at its normal times the
  luxel visibility plane, and grid lighting times the probes' visibility (side file v3, B13),
  interpolated like the SH. The visibility is a scalar fraction of escaped rays, not directional,
  and the indirect bake carries no sky radiance, so enhanced sky light has no bounce; that is the
  price of a sky that changes without a rebake.
\item Legacy non-PBR levels run on \texttt{opengl2}. The stock fallback uses the full bake's
  lightmap, grid and vertex colours.
\item Emissive direct light. The indirect bake leaves out direct light from emissive surfaces
  (\texttt{sh-baker} \#66), and runtime direct light covers only the light entities, so
  \texttt{renderer\_sh} gets it from attached \texttt{light\_area} and \texttt{\_proxy} lights (4.5).
  An unattached emitter only glows and bounces there; \texttt{-shbake} warns about it. The emissive
  surfaces themselves still draw through their additive stage.
\item Exit criterion: the town block passes the stock check, and \texttt{renderer\_sh} renders all
  three bands on surfaces and lights dynamic models from the SH grid.
\item Editor preview of baked SH stays optional and last. The editor has no lightmap coordinates on
  source geometry, and the engine covers the need.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{4.5 Follow-up: LTC area lights}
C7; needs C6 and A11.
\begin{itemize}
\item The author attaches lights in the editor (2.2 step 6, revision 7): a \texttt{light\_area} on
  a static emissive quad, or a \texttt{\_proxy} \texttt{light}/\texttt{light\_spot} on anything

\item \texttt{\_frustum} gives the area light a shadow map from the rectangle's centre, with the

\item The LTC tables are \texttt{setup/data/tools/gl/ltc\_ggx.bin} (BSD), embedded in the module

\item Optional, enhanced-only: \texttt{renderer\_sh} may modulate the area light by the shader's

\subsection{4.6 Follow-up: less noise per sample}
\texttt{sh-baker}'s \texttt{GEMINI.md} adds two phases against bake noise: Phase 10, reusable path
guiding, and Phase 11, OIDN denoising of indirect SH lightmaps. Both are written for
\texttt{sh-baker}'s own glTF and xatlas pipeline (charts, an atlas, EXR output). Under
\texttt{-shbake}, q3map2 owns the mapping, so each phase splits into a library part in
\texttt{sh-baker} (B7, B9) and a q3map2 part (B8, B10). The side file's L0 error (119\%, 22\% and
7\% for the three presets, 4.3) is the noise that matters; the final preset's stock lightmap is
already at the 8-bit floor. Both passes are opt-in. Without them, the output is byte-identical to
today's.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Guiding in \texttt{sh-baker}} (B7). The point bake gets the guide as optional input,
  keyed by caller-defined entries instead of lightmap UVs:
  \begin{itemize}
  \item a \texttt{PathGuide} type with no file format: 16 equal-solid-angle bins per entry, a frame,
    and a chart id for validation;
  \item a point-to-entry map, where $-1$ means unguided;
  \item \texttt{BakeConfig::seed}, so training and production draw independent streams. Today the
    stream is fixed at $12345+i$ (\texttt{baker.cpp:186}).
  \end{itemize}
  The sensor ray mixes the guide with the uniform sampler and uses per-ray mixture densities,
  sky escapes included. Reuse at the first diffuse hit needs the hit's identity: \texttt{Occlusion}
  carries the geometry, primitive and barycentrics, and \texttt{Q3Map2Surface} gains optional guide
  UVs and a chart id. Training is a library call, \texttt{TrainPathGuide}. Phase 10's
  \texttt{sh\_guide\_main} and atlas file are the CLI's own layer on top. One PR per step, reviewed
  as before.
\item \textbf{\texttt{-shguide} in q3map2} (B8).
  \begin{itemize}
  \item Entries are luxels: the $N^2$ super-luxels of one luxel pool into one entry, and a raw
    lightmap is a chart. A super-luxel block never straddles two raw lightmaps, so Phase 10's
    mixed-chart blocks do not arise.
  \item Grid and vertex points stay unguided.
  \item Draw surfaces pass their raw-lightmap super-luxel coordinates as guide UVs. Hits on surfaces
    without a lightmap fall back to cosine sampling.
  \item The guide is trained in-process before the bakes. One guide serves both bakes, because their
    sensor rays and bounces are the same and only the full bake's NEE at the point differs.
  \item An optional cache, \texttt{maps/<name>/<name>.shguide}, is fingerprinted by the draw
    surfaces and the raw lightmap layout. It is never packed into the pk3.
  \item Exit: the preset table of 4.3 remeasured with guiding on, plus the time to train, on the
    benchmark and on a hard indoor map; the stock check passes.
  \end{itemize}
\item \textbf{Denoising in \texttt{sh-baker}} (B9). OIDN's \texttt{RTLightmap} on the CPU, as an
  optional CMake dependency (Apache-2.0, compatible with the GPLv3 that \texttt{SH\_BAKER=1} already
  implies).
  \begin{itemize}
  \item An opaque \texttt{SHDenoiser} owns the device and the scratch buffers, so the OIDN headers
    stay out of the public ones, as with Embree.
  \item \texttt{DenoiseSHCrop} takes one caller-supplied crop with a validity mask. It pads the crop
    from the chart's own values and filters L0 in HDR mode. It filters L1 and L2 as ratios to L0 in
    directional mode, then multiplies them back, and a black crop stays black. It writes back valid
    texels only.
  \item Variants: irradiance alone, and a query for whether the library was built with OIDN.
  \item Errors come back as values, because q3map2 builds without exceptions.
  \end{itemize}
\item \textbf{\texttt{-shdenoise} in q3map2} (B10).
  \begin{itemize}
  \item Each raw lightmap is a crop. The pass runs after the super-sample downsample and before the
    pages are assembled, in \texttt{StoreSurfaceLightmaps}, on the indirect bake's luxels.
  \item Raw lightmaps are small, often $16\times16$ or less, so per-crop overhead and quality are
    measured first. Filtering at super-luxel resolution in \texttt{ShBakeWorld} is the fallback.
  \item Grid and vertex points are not crops and stay raw.
  \item Denoising the full bake's irradiance, the stock lightmap with it, is a
    separate opt-in judged by 0.3 at the draft preset.
  \item q3map2 refuses \texttt{-shdenoise} when the library lacks OIDN.
  \item Exit: Phase 11.4's measurements on q3map2's output, against the 4096-sample references of
    4.3, with guiding off and on: signed L1/L2 relit under several normals, dark rooms, doorway
    edges and neighbouring raw lightmaps, and peak memory against the 32\,GB budget. The stock
    check passes.
  \end{itemize}
\end{itemize}

\section{Units and colour space (cross-cutting)}\label{sec:units}

\textbf{Decided (revision 6): the editor's physical light model is the one light model.} The
editor preview, \texttt{sh-baker} (through \texttt{-shbake}) and \texttt{renderer\_sh} read the same
entities the same way. Nothing reproduces q3map2's \texttt{-light}, and there is no conversion
constant between consumers.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Point.} \texttt{light}: \texttt{intensity} is radiant flux (default 50000); radiant
  intensity is $\texttt{intensity}/4\pi$, falling off with the inverse square of the distance in raw
  map units; culled beyond \texttt{radius} (default 256). Never casts shadows in the preview or at
  runtime.
\item \textbf{Spot.} \texttt{light\_spot}: the same flux concentrated in the cone, radiant intensity
  $\texttt{intensity}/(2\pi(1-\cos\texttt{cone}))$; \texttt{cone} is the outer half-angle (default
  45°), \texttt{cone\_inner} the inner one (default 30°), smoothstep between; direction from
  \texttt{target}, else \texttt{angles}; culled beyond \texttt{radius}.
\item \textbf{Sun.} \texttt{light\_sun}: \texttt{intensity} is the irradiance on a surface facing it
  (default 3), direction from \texttt{angles}, no cutoff. One sun is honoured, the first in map
  order. \texttt{q3map\_sun} is not read.
\item \textbf{Area} (revision 7). \texttt{light\_area}: a one-sided rectangle centred on the
  origin, emitting along the forward vector of \texttt{angles}, \texttt{width} along its right
  vector and \texttt{height} along its up vector, in map units. \texttt{intensity} is the radiance
  $L$, uniform over the rectangle. That fits the point model: on the normal its radiant intensity is
  $LA$, so at distance $d$ it gives $E \approx LA/d^2$, and its flux is $\pi LA$. Direct light is the
  LTC integral over the rectangle (Heitz et al.\ 2016), clipped to the emitting side: the exact
  form factor for diffuse and GGX through the LTC tables for specular, the same tables in the editor
  and \texttt{renderer\_sh}. Culled beyond \texttt{radius} (default 256). The derived radiance is
  that of §\ref{sec:rev7}.
\item \textbf{Proxy} (revision 7). \texttt{\_proxy 1} on a \texttt{light} or \texttt{light\_spot}
  marks it as standing in for an emissive surface; the preview and \texttt{renderer\_sh} treat it as
  an ordinary point or spot, and the bake ignores it, as it ignores \texttt{light\_area}.
\item \textbf{Emissive surfaces.} Every target draws a surface's own radiance (§\ref{sec:materials}).
  It lights other surfaces only through the bake (the full bake directly and by bounce, the
  indirect bake by bounce) and, in the preview and at runtime, through an attached light.
\item \textbf{Sky} (revision 8). The map's sky shader, when it has
  \texttt{qer\_pbr\_emissiveStrength}, is radiance arriving from every direction the sky covers:
  strength $\times$ \texttt{rgbGen const} $\times$ the sky image's linear texel, in the same units
  as emissive surfaces, projected to L0--L2 SH. It never casts. §\ref{sec:sky}.
\item \textbf{Colour and shadows.} \texttt{\_color} is linear RGB. In the editor preview and
  \texttt{renderer\_sh}'s runtime direct light, spots and suns cast unless \texttt{\_shadows 0},
  area lights cast only with a non-zero \texttt{\_frustum}, and point lights never cast. The bake shadows every light, point lights and \texttt{\_shadows 0}
  lights included, because \texttt{sh-baker} has no per-light shadow switch. This is a documented
  difference, with no warning: it changes the stock lightmap's direct light and, slightly, bounced
  light. 0.3 judges it.
\item \textbf{Lightmap encoding.} \texttt{-shbake} converts the full bake's irradiance to the 8-bit
  lightmap lump.
  The stock renderers at default cvars in a window shift lightmap bytes by 2, so a byte saturates
  at 64; the encoding is chosen to look good there, for example a roll-off toward that limit rather
  than a hard clip. It is recorded with the stage and judged by 0.3.
\item \textbf{The placeholder.} q3map2's own reading of light entities (its $\times 7500$
  intensity, distance clamp and envelope) affects only the placeholder lightmap before 4.3 and is
  not matched.
\end{itemize}

\section{Sky light (cross-cutting)}\label{sec:sky}

\textbf{Decided (revision 8): the sky emits; the indirect bake stores only its visibility.} Until now no track had sky light. The point bake read
no environment, so a ray that escaped returned zero. The editor preview has only the constant
``Lighting ambient'' (default 0.02); it draws the sky shader with its skybox program but takes no
light from it. sh-renderer's term (\texttt{radiance.frag:423--424}) is \texttt{u\_sky\_color}
$\times$ sun intensity$/10$ $\times$ baked visibility, a flat colour rather than sky SH.
\texttt{sh-baker}'s \texttt{Scene} already has an optional \texttt{Environment} (texture or
Preetham, \texttt{SHCoeffs}, \texttt{ProjectEnvironmentToSH}; \texttt{scene.h:89--114},
\texttt{:145--146}), which \texttt{-shbake} does not fill. The details below are decided in the
openspec change and the PRs.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Source.} The map's sky shader (\texttt{surfaceparm sky}). Its radiance is
  $\texttt{qer\_pbr\_emissiveStrength} \times \texttt{rgbGen const} \times$ the sky image in
  linear --- the six faces of the \texttt{skyparms} farbox, or the cloud stage's image when there
  is no farbox --- projected to L0--L2 SH. This is the emitter derivation of §\ref{sec:rev7} and the
  radiance units of §\ref{sec:units}. A sky shader without \texttt{qer\_pbr\_emissiveStrength}
  emits nothing, so legacy maps are unchanged. One sky per map; a map that uses more than one sky
  shader gets a warning.
\item \textbf{The town and the benchmark.} The town's sky is \texttt{skyparms - 512 -} with the
  stage \texttt{map textures/town/sky} (\texttt{content/town/scripts/town.shader:320--323}), so its
  radiance comes from the cloud stage's image, whose colour equals the fog's. The benchmark's is
  \texttt{skyparms env/xnight2 - -}, a farbox.
\item \textbf{Editor} (A12, openspec change; needs A1 and A6). The parser reads the sky shader's
  emission, and the preview adds the sky's SH irradiance at the normal, times base colour and
  occlusion. It is unshadowed: the editor has no visibility, so this overlights interiors. The
  constant ``Lighting ambient'' stays for the darkness budget; how the two combine is decided in the
  change.
\item \textbf{Indirect bake and runtime} (B13, C6). The indirect bake never adds sky radiance. It
  records only sky visibility, the fraction of a point's first rays that escape
  (\texttt{baker.cpp:202,231}): per luxel in the side file's visibility plane (already written),
  and per grid probe from side file v3 (B13, q3map2 only). Sky surfaces stay non-casting, so rays
  pass through the town's low sky ceiling. \texttt{renderer\_sh} projects the sky shader to SH at
  load (C6) and adds its irradiance at the normal times that visibility, replacing sh-renderer's
  flat term. The sky can change, even per frame, without a rebake. Enhanced sky light is therefore
  unbounced and its occlusion non-directional; accepted.
\item \textbf{Full bake} (B11 \texttt{sh-baker} PR, B12 q3map2; B12 needs A12 for the shared
  derivation). The point bake returns the environment's radiance on rays that escape, at the point
  and after bounces, unless \texttt{indirect\_only}. \texttt{-shbake} builds the
  \texttt{Environment} from the sky shader and passes it to the full bake only, so the stock
  lightmap, vertex colours and grid carry direct and bounced sky light. A sky change needs a rebake
  for the stock target only. Adding sky light to the lightmap in q3map2 after the bake was rejected:
  it would lose the bounce.
\item \textbf{Parity.} None for sky light: the preview's is unshadowed, the runtime's uses baked
  visibility. 3.4's parity check runs with sky emission off, as the README procedure already does.
\end{itemize}

\section{World-building conventions for the town}

Authoring rules, not code, but they determine whether the visibility system works and whether the
reference look is reachable.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Low sky ceiling just above the camera.} VIS treats sky as opaque, so the town becomes
  a network of corridors walled by buildings and the PVS behaves like an indoor level.
\item \textbf{Fog colour equals sky colour.} Buildings end at the ceiling; with matched colours both
  fade to the same value and the seam disappears. Far plane at fog opacity distance.
\item \textbf{Building shells are structural brushes.} Only structural brushes block VIS. Ornament is
  detail. Props, pipes and clutter are models.
\item \textbf{Hint brushes across long streets} at intervals so far clusters drop out.
\item \textbf{Doors teleport to sealed interiors}, as in the reference game: a static door with a
  \texttt{trigger\_teleport} in front of it, and the room built elsewhere with no opening to the
  street. A sealed room shares no portal with the street, so the PVS hides it wherever its door is.
  A \texttt{func\_door} with an area portal does not: VIS ignores doors, so a room near a junction
  stays in the PVS of far street clusters, and the closed door's hull blocks the bake. A room that
  must be lit from the street keeps a plain open doorway instead. The stock teleport plays its
  sound and flash; a quiet transition needs the game-code decision.
\item \textbf{Clamp camera pitch} so the ceiling is never inspected from below.
\item \textbf{Set worldspawn \texttt{\_blocksize}} to zero or a large value for the flat town, to
  avoid pointless axial splits.
\item \textbf{Silhouette over detail.} At reference fog density, only near-field surfaces are
  legible; budget texture and geometry effort into the first few metres and into readable
  silhouettes at the fog limit.
\item \textbf{Author for a moving light.} Surfaces are seen by a flashlight cone at grazing angles,
  which is the worst case for normal maps and the best case for them to matter. Judge materials
  under the carried light, not under the sun.
\item \textbf{Lightmapped surfaces only}, so every surface receives SH and baked light (4.3).
\item \textbf{Vegetation is lightmapped \texttt{misc\_model} meshes.} Model triangles are vertex-lit
  by default, so vegetation shaders carry \texttt{q3map\_forceMeta}. Grass is static crossed quads, not
  \texttt{autosprite}, which only the engine would turn to the camera.
\item \textbf{Use a spot where occlusion matters.} Point lights do not cast shadows in the editor
  preview or at runtime; a \texttt{light\_spot} does. (The bake shadows both.)
\item \textbf{Stock content only.} Every entity, key and shader keyword obeys
  §\ref{sec:contract} and §\ref{sec:materials}; the stock check is the arbiter.
\end{itemize}
