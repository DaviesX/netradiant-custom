\section{Goal}

Build a playable Silent Hill 1 style town level --- a fog-bound network of watertight streets
under a low virtual sky, with enterable interiors --- that loads in the customised ioquake3 as
\textbf{ordinary Quake 3 content}, and extend the authoring and rendering pipeline until it looks
the way the reference does.

The level is a Quake~3 map, not a new game. One engine binary, one gamepack, one BSP format, stock
\texttt{scripts/*.shader} materials, and the editor's physical light entities. It is a \textbf{new} level, authored
from the start with the enhancements in mind; enhancing existing Quake~3 levels is not part of this
plan (§\ref{sec:prior}).

The compiled level has three targets, in order of precedence:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Authoring.} NetRadiant-custom under \texttt{Q3.game} creates and edits it, including
  its PBR materials as text, and q3map2 compiles it, with no other gamepack involved.
\item \textbf{Stock.} An unmodified ioquake3, with \texttt{opengl1} or its default
  \texttt{opengl2}, at default cvars runs the same pk3 as plain lightmapped Quake~3 content:
  a lightmap baked by \texttt{sh-baker} inside q3map2 (Stage~4), base textures, vanilla shader
  stages, fog volumes. It uses \textbf{no rend2
  features} --- no normal or specular maps, no \texttt{r\_pbr}, no HDR lightmaps. This is what makes
  the level uploadable to \texttt{lvlworld.com}, and it should look as faithful to the enhanced
  target as a lightmap allows.
\item \textbf{Enhanced.} The customised ioquake3 with \texttt{renderer\_sh} (the new sh-renderer)
  runs the same pk3 with direct PBR, shadow maps and SH indirect. PBR levels are developed for
  this target.
\end{enumerate}
Original, non-PBR Quake~3 levels are unaffected: they keep running on \texttt{opengl2}. Every
enhancement is ignored by the stock target. None replaces stock data. §\ref{sec:contract} turns this
into rules every stage must satisfy.

\section{What changed in revision 6}\label{sec:rev6}

The compiler does not have to reproduce any original look: there are no original levels to
rebuild, and what ships is the pk3. It must run on vanilla \texttt{opengl1}/\texttt{opengl2} and
carry everything \texttt{renderer\_sh} needs. Within that, the easiest path in the editor wins.

\begin{description}[style=nextline]
\item[Lights: the editor's physical model, not q3map2's.] The light entities the editor already
  previews --- \texttt{light} (point; \texttt{intensity} radiant flux, \texttt{radius} cutoff),
  \texttt{light\_spot} (\texttt{intensity}, \texttt{radius}, \texttt{cone}, \texttt{cone\_inner},
  \texttt{target} or \texttt{angles}) and \texttt{light\_sun} (irradiance \texttt{intensity},
  \texttt{angles}), with \texttt{\_shadows} on spots and suns --- are the one light model, shared by
  the editor preview, \texttt{sh-baker} and \texttt{renderer\_sh}. Point lights never cast; one sun
  is honoured. Keys keep their names. There is no q3map2 light model to copy, no constant $K$ and
  no calibration against q3map2 lightmaps. The editor does not read \texttt{q3map\_sun}.
  §\ref{sec:units}.
\item[\texttt{Q3.game} gets the PBR entity module (A6).] A tracked overlay in
  \texttt{setup/data/gamepacks/pbr/} installs \texttt{games/Q3.game} --- the downloaded one with
  \texttt{entities="pbr"} --- and \texttt{Q3.game/baseq3/\_pbr\_lights.ent}, defining
  \texttt{light}, \texttt{light\_spot} and \texttt{light\_sun} with the physical keys. Its name
  sorts before \texttt{entities.ent}, and the first entity class of a name is kept, so its
  \texttt{light} wins. It is installed with \texttt{sh install-gamepack.sh setup/data/gamepacks/pbr
  install/gamepacks} after the downloaded packs. A6 is folded into
  \texttt{q3-shader-pbr-materials}.
\item[Vanilla compatibility of the light entities.] The stock game prints \texttt{light\_spot
  doesn't have a spawn function} (and the same for \texttt{light\_sun}) once per entity
  (\texttt{code/game/g\_spawn.c:281}) and discards it; \texttt{light} is freed silently by
  \texttt{SP\_light}. Harmless; the stock check allowlists the line. \texttt{-keeplights} keeps every
  light in the entity lump for \texttt{renderer\_sh}. q3map2 treats any classname prefixed
  \texttt{light} as a light (\texttt{light.cpp:330}), so its placeholder lightmap uses its own
  reading of them.
\item[\texttt{sh-baker} writes the vanilla lightmap.] Stage~4.3 always writes irradiance into the
  BSP lightmap pages, plus the light grid and vertex colours, from the same lights, besides the SH
  side file. This is no longer optional or conditional on 0.3. The irradiance-to-byte encoding is
  ours to choose so the result looks good on vanilla, for example a roll-off rather than a hard clip
  at the overbright limit. Until 4.3 lands, q3map2 \texttt{-light} produces a placeholder lightmap
  that is not expected to match the preview. 0.3 measures the \texttt{sh-baker} lightmap after 4.3.
\item[q3map2 stays the host.] BSP, \texttt{-meta}, \texttt{-vis}, lightmap UV pages, light-grid
  points and vertex lists remain q3map2's; only its lighting maths (\texttt{-light}) is replaced.
\item[Emissive strength has the \texttt{.mtr} meaning.] No scale constant $S$ and no
  \texttt{include/pbrunits.h}: emissive radiance is texel $\times$ \texttt{rgbGen const} colour
  $\times$ \texttt{qer\_pbr\_emissiveStrength}, in the same units as \texttt{.mtr}
  \texttt{emissivestrength}.
\item[The editor's shadow-caster rule is authoritative.] Never cast: sky, fog, hint, areaportal,
  \texttt{noshadows}, water, slime, lava, and \texttt{trans} without \texttt{alphashadow}. Solid
  caulk casts; alpha-tested shaders cast through their preview alpha test; other drawn opaque
  surfaces cast. \texttt{sh-baker} follows the editor; nothing claims to mirror q3map2.
\item[\texttt{pbr.game} stays frozen; its light entities move.] A7 still deletes \texttt{pbr.game}
  and the \texttt{.mtr} parser but keeps the \texttt{pbr} entity module, which \texttt{Q3.game}
  now uses.
\item[Work tracking.] \texttt{q3-stock-lights} is deleted. The changes are
  \texttt{q3-shader-pbr-materials} (A1--A3, A6) and \texttt{q3-bench-and-stock-check} (A4, A5). The
  benchmark keeps its lights as they are; only \texttt{bench.mtr} becomes
  \texttt{scripts/bench.shader}, with no \texttt{q3map\_sun} conversion and no brightness re-tune in
  the stock engine (brightness is tuned in the editor preview). The stock check drops calibration
  mode. It still compiles with \texttt{-meta -keeplights}, \texttt{-vis -saveprt}, \texttt{-light
  -patchshadows}; the last gives the placeholder lightmap.
\item[Engine parity.] 3.4 is parity with the editor's physical light model. The \texttt{-shbake}
  stage learns \texttt{qer\_pbr\_*} and the editor's caster rule.
\end{description}

\section{What changed in revision 5}\label{sec:rev5}

Kept for the record. Revision 6 supersedes its light decisions --- q3map2's model, the constant
$K$ and its calibration, \texttt{q3map\_sun} suns, \texttt{\_sh\_shadows} and the emissive scale
$S$ --- along with the stock-engine brightness tuning of the benchmark, the optional lightmap write
and the \texttt{q3-stock-lights} change (§\ref{sec:rev6}).

\begin{description}[style=nextline]
\item[PBR levels target sh-renderer only; rend2's PBR features are not used.]
  rend2 is built around the lightmap and cannot run a BRDF over metallic--roughness, occlusion and
  normal maps the way sh-renderer does. Revisions 3 and 4 shipped data for it anyway (a
  \texttt{normalMap} stage, a generated \texttt{\_s} pack, \texttt{lm\_*.hdr} calibrated to rend2).
  That is withdrawn. PBR data lives only in top-level \texttt{qer\_pbr\_*} keywords, including the
  normal map (\texttt{qer\_pbr\_normal}), and shader stages stay vanilla. Original non-PBR levels
  fall back to \texttt{opengl2} as before.
\item[The stock target is the lvlworld fallback, and \texttt{opengl1} is back in.]
  With no rend2 keywords in shader text, \texttt{renderergl1} no longer rejects the shaders, so the
  stock check runs both \texttt{opengl1} and \texttt{opengl2} at default cvars. Its fidelity comes
  from q3map2's lightmaps; §0.3 measures how close it gets.
\item[One pk3.] The base/overlay split and the rend2-PBR pack are dropped. One pk3 carries the BSP,
  shaders, base textures, lightmaps, PBR maps and, later, the SH side file. Stock renderers never
  reference the PBR files.
\item[The normal-map syntax of revision 4 was wrong anyway.] rend2 has no \texttt{normalMap
  <image>} keyword: a normal map is a stage containing \texttt{stage normalMap} and \texttt{map
  <image>}, and the bare form makes rend2 reject the shader (\texttt{renderergl2/tr\_shader.cpp:870},
  \texttt{:1267}).
\item[Lights: q3map2's model, exactly, scaled by one constant.] The editor preview reproduces
  q3map2's falloff, spot cone and suns and multiplies by one constant $K$ derived from the lightmap
  path; \texttt{renderer\_sh} adopts the same model (3.4). q3map2's \texttt{light}$\times 7500$ is a
  radiant \emph{intensity}, not a flux. Point lights do not cast shadows in the preview. The
  per-light shadow switch is \texttt{\_sh\_shadows}. §\ref{sec:units}.
\item[Compile flags.] \texttt{-keeplights} is a BSP-stage option (\texttt{bsp.cpp:658}), not a light
  option; \texttt{-patchshadows} is added to \texttt{-light} because q3map2's Quake~3 default leaves
  patches unshadowed while the preview's patches cast.
\item[Work tracking.] Tasks A1--A6 are specified as three openspec changes:
  \texttt{q3-shader-pbr-materials} (A1--A3), \texttt{q3-bench-and-stock-check} (A4--A5) and
  \texttt{q3-stock-lights} (A6).
\end{description}

\section{What changed in revision 4}\label{sec:rev4}

Kept for the record. Revision 5 supersedes its use of rend2's \texttt{normalMap} stage, the
overlay pk3 and \texttt{lm\_*.hdr} (§\ref{sec:rev5}); revision 6 supersedes its handing of q3map2's
parsed lights to the baker (§\ref{sec:rev6}).

\begin{description}[style=nextline]
\item[The export pipeline was a prior attempt, not the pipeline.]
  \texttt{ioq3-map-exporter} and \texttt{ioq3-materialize} were built to export existing Quake~3
  levels and enhance them with no editor in the loop. That did not work well: those levels are
  inherently incompatible with the modern pipeline. Revisions 2 and 3 treated both tools as live
  consumers and drew conclusions from them --- that \texttt{.mtr} ``has no consumer'', that PBR
  maps would be generated and found by file-name suffix, that the exporter's light scale was part
  of the chain. Those conclusions are withdrawn. The move onto Quake~3 shader conventions stands,
  for a different and stronger reason: the stock target cannot load anything else.
\item[PBR materials are authored as text in \texttt{.shader} files.]
  As original NetRadiant does for Quake~3 shaders, with no material GUI. Roughness, metallic,
  occlusion and their factors are top-level \texttt{qer\_pbr\_*} keywords; the normal map is rend2's
  own \texttt{normalMap} stage; base colour and emissive are ordinary stages. §\ref{sec:materials}.
\item[The prefix is \texttt{qer\_pbr\_}.]
  ``qer'' is QuakeEd Radiant, the namespace for editor hints. Both ioquake3 renderers skip any
  top-level keyword starting with \texttt{qer} (\texttt{renderergl2/tr\_shader.cpp:1718}), and
  q3map2 ignores unknown \texttt{qer\_} keywords while it warns on unknown \texttt{q3map\_} ones
  (\texttt{shaders.cpp}, ``Unknown q3map\_* directive''). The keywords are read by the editor,
  q3map2's SH stage and \texttt{renderer\_sh}; the prefix is chosen because stock engines skip it.
\item[The suffix convention of revision 3 is withdrawn.]
  Maps are named explicitly in shader text. What remains is a naming hazard: stock rend2
  auto-loads \texttt{<image>\_n}, \texttt{\_nh} and \texttt{\_s} siblings even without a keyword,
  so those suffixes are reserved (§\ref{sec:materials}).
\item[SH baking moves into q3map2.]
  The prior pipeline baked SH into a single xatlas atlas over an exported glTF and rendered that
  glTF; nothing ever mapped back to the BSP. Revision 4 links \texttt{sh-baker} into q3map2 as a
  library and bakes into q3map2's own lightmap pages, through a new \texttt{-shbake} stage.
  \texttt{sh-baker} gains a library API that serves both xatlas atlases and multi-page layouts,
  delivered as reviewed pull requests to its repository. Stage~4 is rewritten.
\item[The exporter's light conversion leaves the chain.]
  q3map2 hands its own parsed lights to the baker, so the q3map2 interpretation reaches the bake
  directly. The units section drops the exporter row.
\item[Open: parser reuse for \texttt{renderer\_sh}.]
  Revision 2 planned for \texttt{renderer\_sh} to consume the exporter's parsing code
  (\texttt{libioq3\_map}). With the exporter out of the pipeline this is a decision, not a given.
  §\ref{sec:open}.
\end{description}

\textbf{Packaging.} One pk3 per level, as uploaded to \texttt{lvlworld.com}: the BSP, the

is download size.

\subsection{Verification}\label{sec:verify}

The stock check (\texttt{tools/stockcheck/}) runs the fork's ioquake3 build
(\texttt{ioq3-custom/ioq3/build/Release}). Its renderers were machine-converted to C++ and are
otherwise original; each run records the fork's commit so drift is traceable. Matrix:
\texttt{cl\_renderer opengl1} and \texttt{opengl2}, default cvars. Each run uses a clean temporary
basepath and homepath holding only the stock paks and the pk3, \texttt{+set developer 1 +set
logfile 2 +devmap <map>}, then for each documented view (\texttt{maps/<map>.cameras})
\texttt{setviewpos}, a settle wait, \texttt{viewpos} (the reported eye position is recorded in
\texttt{views.txt}, because \texttt{setviewpos} pushes the player forward) and
\texttt{screenshot}. It fails on any \texttt{WARNING} line or \texttt{<classname> doesn't have a
spawn function} line not on a reviewed allowlist (which starts with the
\texttt{light\_spot}/\texttt{light\_sun} spawn-function lines), on \texttt{R\_FindImageFile could
not find}, and on \texttt{Couldn't find image file for shader} (\texttt{developer} only,
\texttt{tr\_shader.cpp:3336}). A missing image is excused only by an allowlist entry naming the
exact line of an image the engine replaces with a built-in fallback (\texttt{renderergl2}'s
\texttt{gfx/2d/sunflare}). It also fails on a missing screenshot, on a \texttt{viewpos} that is
missing or more than 256 units from its request (an ignored teleport), on an abnormal exit and on
a timeout. The compile (\texttt{-meta -keeplights}, \texttt{-vis -saveprt}, \texttt{-light
-patchshadows}) fails on a leak or on q3map2's \texttt{Unknown q3map\_* directive}. The engine only parses shaders a map uses, so the check covers exactly the
shaders the map references. The enhanced check is the same run with \texttt{cl\_renderer sh}.
There is no calibration mode: nothing is matched against q3map2's lightmap.

\subsection{Open decisions}\label{sec:open}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Game code.} baseq3 needs nothing built but has no flashlight on any target. A small
  mod's cgame can attach a light to the player with \texttt{trap\_R\_AddLightToScene} on every
  engine, stock included; that call is omni-only (\texttt{tr\_public.h:75}: origin, intensity,
  colour), so a spot flashlight needs a \texttt{refexport\_t} extension that only the customised
  engine has, with the omni light as the stock fallback. The BSP and pk3 rules do not depend on the
  choice.
\item \textbf{BSP and shader parsing in \texttt{renderer\_sh}.} Reuse the exporter's parsing code
  (\texttt{libioq3\_map}: BSP lumps, patch triangulation, shader scripts, entities), or write the
  runtime's own. The first is less code; the second avoids carrying a library whose parent tool is
  out of the pipeline. q3map2's SH stage does not depend on this: it uses q3map2's own parsing.
\end{itemize}

\section{Material authoring}\label{sec:materials}

A PBR material is an ordinary lightmapped Quake~3 shader, written in \texttt{scripts/*.shader} in
the editor's Shader Editor or any text editor. Its stages are vanilla; all PBR data is in top-level
\texttt{qer\_pbr\_*} keywords, which every stock renderer skips:

\begin{verbatim}
textures/town/brick
{
    qer_editorimage textures/town/brick_c
    qer_pbr_normal textures/town/brick_nrm             // tangent space, linear
    qer_pbr_metallicRoughness textures/town/brick_mr   // G = roughness, B = metallic
    qer_pbr_occlusion textures/town/brick_ao           // R = occlusion
    qer_pbr_roughnessFactor 0.8
    q3map_lightmapSampleSize 8
    {
        map $lightmap
        rgbGen identity
    }
    {
        map textures/town/brick_c          // base colour
        blendFunc GL_DST_COLOR GL_ZERO
        rgbGen identity
    }
}
\end{verbatim}

\begin{tabular}{@{}p{0.30\textwidth}p{0.42\textwidth}p{0.22\textwidth}@{}}
\toprule
Property & Written as & \texttt{.mtr} equivalent \\
\midrule
Base colour (sRGB) & The image of the stage the lightmap multiplies. Stages are written
  explicitly. & \texttt{basecolor} \\
Normal (linear) & \texttt{qer\_pbr\_normal <image>}: tangent space, OpenGL Y-up. & \texttt{normal} \\
Metallic--roughness (linear) & \texttt{qer\_pbr\_metallicRoughness <image>}: G roughness,
  B metallic. Without it, metallic is \texttt{qer\_pbr\_metallicFactor} (default 0) and roughness
  is \texttt{qer\_pbr\_roughnessFactor} (default 1). & \texttt{metallicroughness} \\
Occlusion (linear) & \texttt{qer\_pbr\_occlusion <image>}: R occlusion. & \texttt{occlusion} \\
Factors & \texttt{qer\_pbr\_roughnessFactor <f>}, \texttt{qer\_pbr\_metallicFactor <f>},
  \texttt{qer\_pbr\_baseColorFactor <r> <g> <b>}. With a metallic--roughness map they default to
  1 and multiply it, as in glTF. &
  \texttt{*factor} \\
Emissive (sRGB) & An additive stage (\texttt{blendFunc add}) carrying the emission image, optional
  \texttt{rgbGen const}. \texttt{qer\_pbr\_emissiveStrength <f>} (default 1) scales it with the
  \texttt{.mtr} meaning: radiance $=$ texel $\times$ colour $\times$ strength. Stock renderers draw
  the stage unscaled. &
  \texttt{emissive} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Why emissive is a stage.} An additive stage is how Quake~3 already draws glow, so the
  stock target shows it unchanged; \texttt{sh-baker} already bakes additive layers as area lights
  (``Bake additive SH\_material\_layers surfaces as area lights'', \texttt{843237b}), so the glow
  also reaches the lightmap once 4.3 lands; \texttt{q3map\_surfacelight} is not needed.
\item \textbf{Factors are enhanced-only.} The stock target never sees \texttt{qer\_pbr\_baseColorFactor};
  where the stock look matters, bake the factor into the texture instead.
\item \textbf{Reserved suffixes.} Never name an image \texttt{<base>\_n}, \texttt{<base>\_nh} or
  \texttt{<base>\_s}, where \texttt{<base>} is a stage image: rend2 picks those up with no keyword
  (\texttt{renderergl2/tr\_shader.cpp:2150--2215}). The example above uses \texttt{\_nrm},
  \texttt{\_mr} and \texttt{\_ao}.
\item \textbf{No rend2 stage keywords.} \texttt{stage normalMap}, \texttt{stage specularMap},
  \texttt{roughness} and the rest are rend2-only and make \texttt{renderergl1} reject the shader.
\item \textbf{Non-PBR shaders.} A lightmapped opaque shader without \texttt{qer\_pbr\_*} keywords
  is shaded in the editor preview (and in \texttt{renderer\_sh}) as a fully rough dielectric with
  its base colour, and \texttt{sh-baker} bakes it the same way.
\item \textbf{Editor support} is Stage~2.2: the Quake~3 shader parser reads all of the above and
  fills the existing \texttt{IShader} PBR accessors, the Shader Editor completes the new keywords,
  and the lighting preview renders the result.
\end{itemize}

\section{Current state (verified)}

\subsection{The engine: ioquake3, renderer modules now C++}

\begin{itemize}
\item The renderer is a runtime-loaded module: \texttt{code/client/cl\_main.c:3184} registers
  \texttt{cl\_renderer} (default \texttt{opengl2}, \texttt{CVAR\_ARCHIVE|CVAR\_LATCH}), builds
  \texttt{renderer\_<name>} and resolves \texttt{GetRefAPI} (\texttt{:3204}). \texttt{renderergl1}
  and \texttt{renderergl2} ship side by side; a new backend is a sibling, not a replacement.
\item \textbf{Both renderer modules are now C++} (\texttt{0135354f}, ``CPP Renderers''), and
  \texttt{tr\_public.h} already carries \texttt{extern "C"} guards (\texttt{:25}, \texttt{:214}).
  The \texttt{refexport\_t}/\texttt{refimport\_t} boundary is therefore usable from C++ directly.
  \textbf{This obsoletes the separate C-ABI wrapper} previously planned for \texttt{sh-renderer}
  (\texttt{ioq3-custom/docs/c\_interface\_design.tex}); only the control inversion --- initialise
  against an existing GL context, expose discrete load/update/render/resize/shutdown calls, point
  the GL loader at the engine's --- remains necessary.
\item \texttt{renderergl2} (rend2) already implements, off or on by cvar in
  \texttt{tr\_init.cpp}: \texttt{r\_pbr} (\texttt{:1241}, default \textbf{0}), \texttt{r\_hdr}
  (\texttt{:1201}, default 1), \texttt{r\_toneMap} (\texttt{:1206}), \texttt{r\_normalMapping} and
  \texttt{r\_specularMapping} (\texttt{:1225--1227}, both default 1), \texttt{r\_deluxeMapping}
  (\texttt{:1229}), \texttt{r\_cubeMapping} (\texttt{:1236}), \texttt{r\_sunlightMode}
  (\texttt{:1271}, cascaded sun shadows), \texttt{r\_dlightMode} (\texttt{:1252}; $\geq 2$ gives
  per-pixel dynamic lights \emph{with} projected shadows), plus SSAO and a tonemap post chain.
\item Under \texttt{USE\_PBR}, \texttt{glsl/lightall\_fp.glsl:398--404} is metallic--roughness with
  $F_0 = \mathrm{mix}(0.04, \text{basecolor}, \text{metallic})$, Disney diffuse and GGX-ish
  specular. Maps come from the stage keywords \texttt{normalMap} (\texttt{tr\_shader.cpp:881}) and
  \texttt{specularMap} (\texttt{:894}), the \texttt{roughness} keyword (\texttt{:976}), or the
  \texttt{\_n}/\texttt{\_nh}/\texttt{\_s} siblings it loads without a keyword (\texttt{:2150--2215}).
\item \textbf{Specular map interpretation depends on client cvars.} With \texttt{r\_pbr 0} (default)
  it is RGB reflectance plus alpha gloss; with \texttt{r\_pbr 1}, red is gloss under
  \texttt{r\_glossType} (default 1, smoothness) and green is metallic. Colour space differs too:
  \texttt{lightall\_fp.glsl:394} uses \texttt{diffuse.rgb *= diffuse.rgb}, a gamma-2.0
  approximation, where the editor uses the exact piecewise sRGB curve.
\item \textbf{rend2 loads external HDR lightmaps}: \texttt{maps/<name>/lm\_\%04d.hdr},
  promoting the lightmap atlas to \texttt{GL\_RGBA16} when the first one exists and \texttt{r\_hdr}
  is on (\texttt{tr\_bsp.cpp:279--326}).
\item \textbf{None of rend2's PBR, specular, normal-map or HDR-lightmap features are used}
  (revision 5). They are listed here because the contract must keep rend2 from picking them up by
  accident.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{The prior attempt}\label{sec:prior}

\texttt{ioq3-map-exporter} (\texttt{.pk3} $\to$ IBSP $\to$ glTF), \texttt{ioq3-materialize}
(Real-ESRGAN upscale, DeepBump normals, ORM packing), \texttt{sh-baker} and \texttt{sh-renderer}
were built to enhance existing levels such as \texttt{q3dm1} and \texttt{mpteam3}. How the bake was
wired, which is why it does not carry over as-is:
\begin{itemize}
\item The exporter writes each vertex's q3map2 lightmap coordinates, which are local to one
  lightmap page, as \texttt{TEXCOORD\_1}.
\item \texttt{sh-baker} ignores them: its loader reads only \texttt{TEXCOORD\_0}
  (\texttt{src/loader.cpp:170}, \texttt{:212}). It builds a fresh atlas with xatlas, splitting
  vertices along chart seams, and fails unless everything fits one page
  (\texttt{src/atlas.cpp:110--207}); \texttt{--skip\_parameterization} still repacks the charts.
  It writes a new glTF with its own \texttt{TEXCOORD\_1} (\texttt{src/saver.cpp:769}).
\item \texttt{sh-renderer} renders that glTF (\texttt{src/loader.cpp:142}, \texttt{:941}), never
  the BSP.
\end{itemize}
\textbf{Carried over:} \texttt{sh-renderer} as \texttt{renderer\_sh} (Stage~3) and \texttt{sh-baker}
as a library linked into q3map2 (Stage~4). \texttt{ioq3-materialize} and the exporter's glTF output
are not part of this plan; the exporter's parsing code is an open decision (§\ref{sec:open}).

\subsection{The editor and q3map2}

\begin{itemize}
\item Gamepacks are data; the lighting draw mode, the half-float target and tonemap resolve, and
  sun-cascade plus spot-atlas shadow maps are implemented and documented
  (\texttt{radiant/renderstate.cpp}, \texttt{radiant/cascade.h},
  \texttt{install/gamepacks/pbr.game/README.md}).
\item The gating on ``is this a PBR game'' is a single string test,
  \texttt{renderstate.cpp:2202}, against the \texttt{.game} \texttt{shaders} key; per-material
  dispatch already exists (\texttt{renderstate.cpp:3054}, \texttt{m\_shader->isPBR()}), so a mixed
  material set already renders correctly.
\item Text authoring of PBR materials exists today only as \texttt{.mtr} under \texttt{pbr.game}
  (\texttt{ShaderTemplate::parsePBR}, \texttt{plugins/shaders/shaders.cpp:1498}), which the stock
  target cannot load. The Shader Editor (\texttt{radiant/gtkdlgs.cpp:599}) highlights and completes
  Quake~3 and q3map2 keywords; its table has none of rend2's.
\item \texttt{Q3.game} already authors and compiles stock maps; it lacks the PBR keywords and the
  lighting preview.
\item q3map2 assigns lightmap coordinates and pages during \texttt{-light}
  (\texttt{SetupSurfaceLightmaps}, \texttt{light.cpp:2964}); they are final after
  \texttt{StoreSurfaceLightmaps} (\texttt{lightmaps\_ydnar.cpp:2253}). It has no \texttt{.hdr}
  writer. It builds with \texttt{-std=c++20 -fno-exceptions -fno-rtti} from the top-level
  \texttt{Makefile} (\texttt{:138}) and is GPL-2.0-or-later (\texttt{q3map2.h:9--11}).
\end{itemize}

\section{Stage 1: The Silent Hill look}

\textbf{Outcome.} The block reads as the reference: near-zero visibility, darkness with a moving
pool of light, no discernible skyline.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Fog is the primary aesthetic, not a VIS trick.} Tune fog volume density and colour
  against the sky, with the far plane at fog opacity distance. Decide in \texttt{renderer\_sh}
  whether fog composites before or after the tonemap --- the reference look depends on which --- and
  keep the stock fallback's \texttt{fogparms} reading close to it.
\item \textbf{Carried flashlight.} A player-attached dynamic spot light, shadow-casting, is the
  defining mechanic. It needs the game-code decision of §\ref{sec:open}. In \texttt{renderer\_sh}
  it is a shadowed spot using the editor's spot atlas scheme; the stock fallback gets the omni
  dynamic light that \texttt{trap\_R\_AddLightToScene} provides.
\item \textbf{Grass and trees.} Overgrown verges and bare trees belong to the town's look. Build
  them as the benchmark does: \texttt{misc\_model} meshes merged into the BSP by q3map2, every
  vegetation shader lightmapped through \texttt{q3map\_forceMeta}, leaf cards alpha-tested and casting
  through their alpha, grass as static crossed quads that cast nothing. Scattering at town scale
  (by hand, or a compile-time scatter the editor cannot preview) is decided here.
\item \textbf{Darkness budget.} With physical units and a tonemap, the ambient floor is what
  separates ``dark and readable'' from ``black''. Fix an exposure and ambient for the town and
  record them next to the map, as the benchmark already does.
\item Exit criterion: a side-by-side against Silent Hill 1 reference stills that the author
  accepts. This is a judgement call, deliberately, and it gates nothing downstream.
\end{itemize}

\section{Stage 2: Retarget the editor onto Quake 3 conventions}

\textbf{Outcome.} One gamepack. PBR materials are authored as text under \texttt{Q3.game}, and the
lighting preview predicts the engine because both read the same data the same way.

\subsection{2.1 Decision: \texttt{pbr.game} is frozen}

\textbf{Decided (revision 3).} No new content is authored in \texttt{pbr.game}; \texttt{Q3.game} is
the only authoring target. A map authored in \texttt{pbr.game} uses \texttt{.mtr} materials, which
the stock target cannot load, so it violates target 1 directly. Its light entities are not the
problem: revision 6 moves them to \texttt{Q3.game} (2.2 step 4). Authoring geometry and shader text
in \texttt{Q3.game} needs no editor change; the PBR keywords, the lighting preview and the light
entities arrive with 2.2.

The retarget is code work, not a \texttt{.game} edit. Module selection at
\texttt{radiant/plugin.cpp:196--199} takes exactly one module per key, and neither assignment
gives both stock shaders and the preview:
\begin{itemize}
\item \texttt{shaders="quake3"} clears \texttt{g\_pbrGame} (\texttt{renderstate.cpp:2202}), and
  with it the PBR shading path (\texttt{:3054}) and both shadow atlases ---
  \texttt{renderstate.cpp:2165} returns early before building them.
\item \texttt{shaders="pbr"} points the loader at \texttt{materials/*.mtr} with
  \texttt{g\_enableDefaultShaders=false}, so stock \texttt{scripts/*.shader} files stop loading
  and bare textures get no fallback shader.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{2.2 The retarget, in landing order}

\item \textbf{Shader Editor completion.} Add the \texttt{qer\_pbr\_*} keywords to the keyword table

\item \textbf{Lighting mode under \texttt{Q3.game}.} Replace the \texttt{renderstate.cpp:2202}

\item \textbf{Light entities under \texttt{Q3.game}} (revision 6; part of the first openspec

\section{Stage 3: \texttt{renderer\_sh} and engine-side parity}

\textbf{Outcome.} A second renderer module that carries the project, then direct PBR and shadows
matching the editor preview.

\subsection{3.1 Decision: build the Quake 3 feature set out in sh-renderer}

\textbf{Decided.} The missing feature set is added to \texttt{sh-renderer}, which becomes
\texttt{renderer\_sh}. \texttt{renderergl2} is left untouched. Rejected: modifying rend2 to host
sh-renderer's SH reconstruction and Forward+ culling.

Rationale:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{The work is additive.} Nearly every item in the inventory below is a new module in a
  codebase whose architecture is already written down and enforced. Hosting SH in rend2 would
  instead mean editing its shader-permutation system, its backend command buffer and its
  \texttt{tess} batching --- where its accumulated complexity lives, and what is hardest to reason
  about from outside.
\item \textbf{The two renderers coexist, so an incomplete one costs nothing.}
  \texttt{cl\_renderer} is a latched cvar: \texttt{renderer\_sh} can lack sky, marks and
  cinematics and still be useful for walking the town and judging light, with \texttt{opengl2} one
  cvar away as an A/B reference on the same map. Modifying rend2 has the opposite property --- a
  half-finished integration degrades a renderer that works today, and that renderer is the stock
  target.
\item \textbf{The architectural directives survive.} GL 4.6, DSA-only, Forward+, SSBOs stay intact
  rather than being recorded as superseded.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{What changes in sh-renderer's inputs.} It renders the BSP, not a glTF: draw surfaces with
their q3map2 lightmap coordinates, materials from the shader text of §\ref{sec:materials}, the SH
bands from the side file of Stage~4. Its glTF loader stays for its own tests and tools. The parser it uses is the open decision of §\ref{sec:open}.

\textbf{The one part that is not additive}, and is planned as a refactor rather than a feature:
\texttt{sh-renderer} currently owns its process. It uses \texttt{glog}, \texttt{gflags}, its own
file I/O, and its own GLFW window and frame loop. As a renderer module it must route logging
through \texttt{ri.Printf}/\texttt{ri.Error}, configuration through \texttt{ri.Cvar\_Get},
\emph{all} file access through \texttt{ri.FS\_ReadFile} so assets come from \texttt{.pk3} via the
engine's VFS, allocation through \texttt{ri.Hunk\_Alloc}, and must initialise against an
already-current GL context with glad pointed at the engine's loader. This touches every module's
edges. Do it first, before any feature work, so no feature is ported twice.

\subsection{3.2 Free: link, do not reimplement}

\texttt{code/renderercommon/} is format and utility code rather than renderer architecture, and is
already compiled into both existing modules: image decoders for TGA, JPG, PNG, BMP, PCX and PVR,
plus \texttt{tr\_font.c}, \texttt{tr\_noise.c}, \texttt{tr\_subs.c} and \texttt{puff.c}. Link it.
\texttt{tr\_public.h} and \texttt{tr\_types.h} define the ABI and are shared by definition.

\subsection{3.3 Feature inventory}

\texttt{refexport\_t} has 37 entry points. Ordered below by whether the town is playable without
the item. The LOC figures are the corresponding rend2 sources, as an order-of-magnitude scope
reference --- not as code to copy.

\textbf{Tier 1 --- required to boot and walk the map.}
\begin{itemize}
\item Module skeleton: \texttt{GetRefAPI}, the \texttt{refexport\_t} table,

\item The \texttt{ri.*} refactor of §3.1.
\item \texttt{LoadWorld} from \texttt{.bsp}, plus \texttt{SetWorldVisData}, \texttt{inPVS} and
  PVS-based cluster culling, and \texttt{GetEntityToken} (cgame reads the entity lump through the

\item Materials from shader text: the base-colour stage, the \texttt{qer\_pbr\_*} keywords,

\item 2D: \texttt{SetColor}, \texttt{DrawStretchPic}, \texttt{RegisterShaderNoMip}. Without these

\item Frame protocol: \texttt{ClearScene}, \texttt{AddRefEntityToScene}, \texttt{RenderScene},

\item MD3: \texttt{RegisterModel}, \texttt{RegisterSkin}, \texttt{ModelBounds},

\textbf{Tier 2 --- playable, but visibly wrong without it.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Sky} (\texttt{tr\_sky}, 859 LOC). Highest priority of this tier for this project

\item \textbf{Fog volumes}, with the stock \texttt{fogparms} semantics so the same map reads the

\item Multi-stage shader runtime: \texttt{tcMod}, \texttt{rgbGen}, blend funcs, \texttt{animMap},

\item \texttt{MarkFragments} --- bullet holes and blood decals (\texttt{tr\_marks}, 467 LOC).
\item \texttt{AddPolyToScene} --- cgame's particle and effect path leans on it heavily.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Tier 3 --- stub or defer.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \texttt{DrawStretchRaw}, \texttt{UploadCinematic}, \texttt{TakeVideoFrame} --- video
  playback and capture.
\item \texttt{RegisterFont}, \texttt{RemapShader}, \texttt{AddAdditiveLightToScene}.
\item Skeletal models, MDR and IQM (\texttt{tr\_model\_iqm}, 1850 LOC). \texttt{baseq3} does not

Exit criterion for Stage 3.3: the town block is walkable under \texttt{cl\_renderer sh} with a
working HUD and player model, screenshots from the documented views can be compared against the
stock fallback on the same map, and the stock check still passes on the same pk3.

\subsection{3.4 Direct light parity}
\begin{itemize}
\item Lights come from the entity lump, preserved by \texttt{-keeplights}, read with the editor's
  physical model (§\ref{sec:units}): \texttt{light}, \texttt{light\_spot}, \texttt{light\_sun} and
  their keys, with no conversion constant. This replaces sh-renderer's glTF light scale
  (\texttt{kLightIntensityScale}, \texttt{src/loader.cpp:18}) and \texttt{ComputeLightRadius}
  (\texttt{src/loader.cpp:719}).
\item Exit criterion: the editor lighting preview and the engine's direct-light-only render agree
  from the documented camera, within the tolerance already written down for the benchmark map.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{3.5 Shadow parity and hull reconstruction}
\begin{itemize}
\item Rebuild each collision brush's convex hull from the brush and brushside lumps at load, and
  use the hulls as front-face-culled casters; draw surfaces remain receivers. No BSP format change:
  the lumps already keep every plane of every solid brush, including void-facing ones.
\item Alpha-tested draw surfaces cast too: surfaces whose shader the editor's caster rule says casts
  through its alpha (the fence, leaf cards; \texttt{trans} with \texttt{alphashadow}) are drawn into the
  shadow maps with their alpha test, alongside the hulls. They are non-solid, so the hulls alone
  would drop them, and the editor and sh-baker already cast them.
\item Known gap: other brushes with non-solid shaders are dropped from the collision lumps. If such a
  brush must cast, mark its material to keep it, or emit casters separately.
\item Reconcile against the editor's scheme, which deliberately uses \emph{first}-depth casters
  with normal-offset and slope-scaled bias rather than second depth, because second depth is
  undefined for patches and models. The constants and their provenance are already tabulated in
  the gamepack README; that table moves with the pack in Stage 2.
\item Exit criterion: the town block's shadows match between editor and engine.
\end{itemize}

\section{Stage 4: SH indirect, baked by q3map2}

\textbf{Outcome.} \texttt{sh-baker}, inside q3map2, bakes the editor's lights into q3map2's own
lightmap pages: SH bands for \texttt{renderer\_sh}, and the stock lightmap, light grid and vertex
colours for the lvlworld build. No BSP format change.

\subsection{4.1 Decision: link \texttt{sh-baker} into q3map2}

\textbf{Decided.} q3map2 links \texttt{sh-baker} as a library and bakes SH into the lightmap pages it
has already allocated, in a new \texttt{-shbake} stage. Rejected:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Exporter, glTF, xatlas atlas} (the prior pipeline). The bake lives in a separate
  atlas over a separate, vertex-split mesh; nothing maps it back to the BSP, so
  \texttt{renderer\_sh} would have to render a second geometry and keep fog, shader stages, PVS
  and patches in step with it.
\item \textbf{Writing xatlas coordinates back into the BSP.} Needs the draw surfaces rewritten to
  hold split vertices and patches converted to triangles (\texttt{-patchmeta}) --- the largest
  option.
\end{itemize}
Grounds: q3map2 already has the BSP, shader parsing, patch tessellation, image loading, the light
entities, lightmap page allocation and light-grid points. The stage passes the editor's light
entities to \texttt{sh-baker} unchanged, so units agree by construction (§\ref{sec:units}). Baking
in q3map2's lightmap space means \texttt{renderer\_sh} uses the BSP's own coordinates with no
mapping file, and the same bake fills the stock lightmap (revision 6).

\subsection{4.2 \texttt{sh-baker} as a library}

Delivered as pull requests to \texttt{sh-baker}, reviewed and merged by its maintainer. Each keeps
the CLI's current behaviour and its tests passing.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Library boundary.} A shared-library target with a public header free of tinygltf,
  Embree, glog and gflags types (Eigen and the standard library only), safe to include under
  \texttt{-fno-exceptions -fno-rtti}. Logging through a caller-supplied callback (110
  \texttt{LOG} calls today). glTF loading and saving become an optional module. No
  \texttt{-march=native} in the library's default build.
\item \textbf{Multi-page UV layouts.} Each geometry carries a lightmap page index; a layout lists
  one size per page; the rasterizer works one page at a time. xatlas becomes one producer of a
  layout and may emit several pages rather than failing when \texttt{atlasCount > 1}
  (\texttt{atlas.cpp:201}). This is what makes one API serve both xatlas and q3map2's pages.
\item \textbf{Bake session.} \texttt{BakeSHLightMap} creates the Embree device and BVH on every
  call (\texttt{baker.cpp:95--96}, released at \texttt{:202--203}). A session builds them and the
  light trees once and bakes page by page, with progress and cancel callbacks.
\item \textbf{Outputs.} The SH side-file format (L0, L1, L2 per page), irradiance per page and at
  given points (for the BSP lightmap, the light grid and vertex colours), plus an optional Radiance
  \texttt{.hdr} dump of L0 for inspection. No rend2 layout.
\end{enumerate}
The existing light and environment types already cover what q3map2 passes in: point, spot,
directional, area lights and a Preetham sky; material-less geometry is already a pure occluder.

\subsection{4.3 The \texttt{-shbake} stage in q3map2}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Placement.} \texttt{q3map2 -meta}, \texttt{-vis}, \texttt{-light}, then
  \texttt{q3map2 -shbake} on the finished BSP. Lightmap coordinates and pages are final only after
  \texttt{StoreSurfaceLightmaps}, so \texttt{-light} still runs to allocate them (its lighting is
  overwritten; \texttt{-light -fast} suffices), and a separate stage keeps the bake out of
  \texttt{light.cpp}'s internals.
\item \textbf{Scene.} Draw surfaces --- planar, meta, triangle soups, and patches tessellated with
  interpolated lightmap coordinates --- each tagged with its lightmap page. Materials from q3map2's
  shader info: the base-colour image, the \texttt{qer\_pbr\_*} maps and factors (q3map2's shader
  parser learns them), additive stages as emitters. Lights are the editor's \texttt{light},
  \texttt{light\_spot} and \texttt{light\_sun} entities read with the physical model of
  §\ref{sec:units}, not q3map2's light parsing. Occluders follow the editor's shadow-caster rule
  (§\ref{sec:rev6}), with solid brush hulls matching 3.5.
\item \textbf{Bake and write.} One session, one bake per page, dilation per page. All bands to
  the side file in \texttt{maps/<name>/}, inside the level's single pk3. Never
  \texttt{lm\_*.hdr}, which stock rend2 would load. Always also write direct plus indirect
  irradiance into the BSP's 8-bit lightmap lump, the light grid (ambient, directed, direction from
  SH at the grid points) and vertex colours, so the lvlworld build carries the same light
  \texttt{renderer\_sh} shows. The irradiance-to-byte encoding is chosen here (§\ref{sec:units}).
\item \textbf{Gaps the stage reports.} q3map2-only light inputs --- \texttt{spawnflags},
  \texttt{fade}, \texttt{q3map\_sun}, \texttt{q3map\_skylight}, \texttt{q3map\_surfacelight} ---
  are not read; the stage warns on them.
\item \textbf{Build.} \texttt{sh-baker} builds with its own CMake and flags; q3map2's
  \texttt{Makefile} links it only under an opt-in flag (\texttt{SH\_BAKER=1}), so a default q3map2
  build needs neither Embree nor TBB. The stage lives in a new \texttt{light\_sh.cpp}.
\item \textbf{Licence.} \texttt{sh-baker} is MIT, compatible with q3map2's GPL-2.0-or-later. Embree
  and TBB are Apache-2.0, compatible with GPLv3 but not GPLv2, so a q3map2 binary built with
  \texttt{SH\_BAKER=1} is distributed under GPLv3, which ``or any later version'' permits.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{4.4 Runtime and exit}
\begin{itemize}
\item \texttt{renderer\_sh} reads the side file and runs the indirect BRDF: Ramamoorthi--Hanrahan
  cosine convolution for diffuse, SH evaluated along the reflection vector for specular. A map
  without the side file falls back to the BSP lightmap.
\item Exit criterion: the town block passes the stock check, and \texttt{renderer\_sh} renders all
  three bands.
\item Editor preview of baked SH stays optional and last. The editor has no lightmap coordinates on
  source geometry, and the engine covers the need.
\end{itemize}

\section{Units and colour space (cross-cutting)}\label{sec:units}

\textbf{Decided (revision 6): the editor's physical light model is the one light model.} The
editor preview, \texttt{sh-baker} (through \texttt{-shbake}) and \texttt{renderer\_sh} read the same
entities the same way. Nothing reproduces q3map2's \texttt{-light}, and there is no conversion
constant between consumers.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Point.} \texttt{light}: \texttt{intensity} is radiant flux (default 50000); radiant
  intensity is $\texttt{intensity}/4\pi$, falling off with the inverse square of the distance in raw
  map units; culled beyond \texttt{radius} (default 256). Never casts shadows.
\item \textbf{Spot.} \texttt{light\_spot}: the same flux concentrated in the cone, radiant intensity
  $\texttt{intensity}/(2\pi(1-\cos\texttt{cone}))$; \texttt{cone} is the outer half-angle (default
  45°), \texttt{cone\_inner} the inner one (default 30°), smoothstep between; direction from
  \texttt{target}, else \texttt{angles}; culled beyond \texttt{radius}.
\item \textbf{Sun.} \texttt{light\_sun}: \texttt{intensity} is the irradiance on a surface facing it
  (default 3), direction from \texttt{angles}, no cutoff. One sun is honoured, the first in map
  order. \texttt{q3map\_sun} is not read.
\item \textbf{Colour and shadows.} \texttt{\_color} is linear RGB. Spots and suns cast unless
  \texttt{\_shadows 0}.
\item \textbf{Lightmap encoding.} \texttt{-shbake} converts irradiance to the 8-bit lightmap lump.
  The stock renderers at default cvars in a window shift lightmap bytes by 2, so a byte saturates
  at 64; the encoding is chosen to look good there, for example a roll-off toward that limit rather
  than a hard clip. It is recorded with the stage and judged by 0.3.
\item \textbf{The placeholder.} q3map2's own reading of light entities (its $\times 7500$
  intensity, distance clamp and envelope) affects only the placeholder lightmap before 4.3 and is
  not matched.
\end{itemize}

\section{World-building conventions for the town}

Authoring rules, not code, but they determine whether the visibility system works and whether the
reference look is reachable.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Low sky ceiling just above the camera.} VIS treats sky as opaque, so the town becomes
  a network of corridors walled by buildings and the PVS behaves like an indoor level.
\item \textbf{Fog colour equals sky colour.} Buildings end at the ceiling; with matched colours both
  fade to the same value and the seam disappears. Far plane at fog opacity distance.
\item \textbf{Building shells are structural brushes.} Only structural brushes block VIS. Ornament is
  detail. Props, pipes and clutter are models.
\item \textbf{Hint brushes across long streets} at intervals so far clusters drop out; area portals
  in doors between street and interiors.
\item \textbf{Clamp camera pitch} so the ceiling is never inspected from below.
\item \textbf{Set worldspawn \texttt{\_blocksize}} to zero or a large value for the flat town, to
  avoid pointless axial splits.
\item \textbf{Silhouette over detail.} At reference fog density, only near-field surfaces are
  legible; budget texture and geometry effort into the first few metres and into readable
  silhouettes at the fog limit.
\item \textbf{Author for a moving light.} Surfaces are seen by a flashlight cone at grazing angles,
  which is the worst case for normal maps and the best case for them to matter. Judge materials
  under the carried light, not under the sun.
\item \textbf{Lightmapped surfaces only}, so every surface receives SH and baked light (4.3).
\item \textbf{Vegetation is lightmapped \texttt{misc\_model} meshes.} Model triangles are vertex-lit
  by default, so vegetation shaders carry \texttt{q3map\_forceMeta}. Grass is static crossed quads, not
  \texttt{autosprite}, which only the engine would turn to the camera.
\item \textbf{Use a spot where occlusion matters.} Point lights do not cast shadows in the editor
  preview; a \texttt{light\_spot} does.
\item \textbf{Stock content only.} Every entity, key and shader keyword obeys
  §\ref{sec:contract} and §\ref{sec:materials}; the stock check is the arbiter.
\end{itemize}
